Few prayers feel more urgent than the ones we pray for our kids. Scripture gives parents real language for this — not vague hopes, but specific requests rooted in verses about a child's heart, protection, and future. Here's how to pray for your children with the Bible as your guide.

## Why Pray for Your Children

Parenting can influence a lot, but it can't control a child's heart — that's between them and God. Prayer is where a parent hands over what they can't control. Scripture treats this as normal, not a last resort: Job "would rise early in the morning and offer burnt offerings" for his children "regularly" (Job 1:5), and Paul prayed constantly for the spiritual growth of the churches he loved like children (Colossians 1:9).

## What the Bible Says About Praying for Children

### Proverbs 22:6

> "Train up a child in the way he should go; even when he is old he will not depart from it." (Proverbs 22:6, ESV)

This verse is often read as a formula, but it's better understood as a proverb — a general truth, not a guarantee. It still points to something real: consistent spiritual investment, prayer included, shapes a child's trajectory.

### 3 John 1:4

> "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children are walking in the truth." (3 John 1:4, ESV)

This wasn't written by a biological parent, but the sentiment fits every parent's deepest hope — not just that a child succeeds, but that they walk in truth.

### Deuteronomy 6:6-7

> "And these words that I command you today shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise." (Deuteronomy 6:6-7, ESV)

Prayer for children works alongside this kind of everyday discipleship — talking about faith in ordinary moments, not just formal teaching.

## A Prayer You Can Pray

*"Lord, I bring [child's name] before you today. Give them a heart that knows and loves you. Protect them from harm, from bad influences, and from their own worst impulses. Give them wisdom beyond their years, courage to do what's right even when it's hard, and friends who point them toward you rather than away. Let them walk in truth all their days. In Jesus's name, amen."*

## Specific Things to Pray For

### Salvation and a Soft Heart

Pray that your child would come to know Christ personally, not just inherit a family religion. Ask God to keep their heart soft and responsive rather than hardened by pride or hurt (Ezekiel 36:26).

### Protection

Pray for physical safety, but also for protection from harmful influences, dangerous choices, and spiritual attack (Psalm 91:11, "For he will command his angels concerning you to guard you in all your ways").

### Wisdom and Discernment

Ask God to give your child wisdom for decisions you won't always be present for — James 1:5 promises that "if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach."

### Friendships and Influence

Pray specifically for the people your child spends time with, since Proverbs 13:20 warns that "whoever walks with the wise becomes wise, but the companion of fools will suffer harm."

### Their Future

Pray over decisions still ahead — school, career, marriage, calling — trusting Jeremiah 29:11: "For I know the plans I have for you, declares the Lord, plans for welfare and not for evil, to give you a future and a hope."

## FAQ

### What is the most powerful Bible verse to pray over your children?

There isn't one single "most powerful" verse — Proverbs 22:6, Jeremiah 29:11, and Psalm 91:11 are among the most commonly prayed over children because they cover formation, future, and protection.

### How often should I pray for my children?

Scripture doesn't set a required frequency, but Job's example of praying "regularly" (Job 1:5) and Paul's language of "constantly" remembering people in prayer (1 Thessalonians 1:2) suggest consistency matters more than length or occasion.

### Can I pray for my adult children the same way?

Yes. The specifics shift — less about daily protection, more about their choices, relationships, and walk with God — but the same posture of surrender and trust applies at any age.

### What if my child has walked away from faith?

Keep praying. The Prodigal Son parable (Luke 15:11-32) pictures a father who never stopped watching for his son's return, and Scripture consistently portrays God as patient and pursuing, not quick to give up on a wandering child.
