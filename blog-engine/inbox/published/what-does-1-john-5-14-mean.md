**Short answer:** 1 John 5:14 says, "And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us" (KJV). John promises believers real boldness in prayer — God genuinely listens — while attaching one qualifier: the request is aligned with what God wills. The verse offers assurance, not a blank check.

## The context: a letter written so you may know

First John was written, John says, so that believers "may know that ye have eternal life" (5:13, KJV). Assurance is the theme of the whole letter, and verse 13 is its summary statement.

Verse 14 begins with "and this is the confidence" — meaning, here is another thing that flows from that assurance. Having settled that his readers possess eternal life, John shows what that opens up: access.

Verse 15 completes the thought: "And if we know that he hear us, whatsoever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we desired of him" (KJV). Verse 16 then gives a concrete example — praying for a brother who sins — which suggests John has intercession particularly in view.

The word translated "confidence" (*parrēsia*) is used elsewhere in this letter for boldness before God at his coming (2:28; 4:17). It originally described a citizen's freedom to speak openly in public assembly. Applied to prayer, it means we are not petitioners hoping to be admitted; we speak freely as those who belong.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

**"This is the confidence that we have in him."** The confidence is *in him* — it rests on God's character and on the standing believers have in Christ, not on the strength of our faith or the eloquence of our praying.

**"If we ask any thing."** The scope is deliberately wide. John does not restrict prayer to a category of approved topics. "Any thing" invites the whole range of human need.

**"According to his will."** The qualifier. This is not a trick clause that quietly cancels the promise. It reflects the fact that God is not a mechanism to be operated. A God who granted every request regardless of its wisdom would be dangerous, not loving.

Praying according to God's will is not the same as guessing correctly. Scripture reveals a great deal of God's will plainly — that we grow in holiness (1 Thessalonians 4:3), that we forgive, that we love. Those requests can be prayed with total certainty. Other matters — a specific outcome, a particular healing, a job — are not revealed, and Jesus himself modeled how to pray them: "not my will, but thine, be done" (Luke 22:42, KJV).

**"He heareth us."** In biblical usage, "hearing" often carries the sense of attending and responding, not merely registering sound. God is not indifferent.

**Where faithful Christians differ:** how to understand unanswered prayer. Some emphasize that a request outside God's will is answered with a loving no. Others emphasize timing, or point to James 4:3 on wrong motives, or to the mystery Paul confronted when his own request was refused three times (2 Corinthians 12:8–9). Traditions also differ on whether believers can discern God's will for specific circumstances or should generally pray with submission. Scripture supports both boldness and submission and does not tidily systematize the tension.

## How to apply it today

Let the promise do its work before the qualifier does. Many Christians read this verse defensively, as an explanation for why prayers fail. John wrote it as encouragement. The headline is that God listens.

Practically, pray the revealed will of God with full confidence. When you ask for wisdom (James 1:5), for forgiveness, for the ability to forgive someone, for growth in holiness, for laborers for the harvest, there is no uncertainty about whether the request matches God's will. Pray those boldly and expectantly.

For the unrevealed — outcomes, healings, decisions — ask specifically and hold the outcome loosely. Both halves matter. Vagueness is not humility, and refusing to submit is not faith. Jesus asked plainly for the cup to pass and then submitted in the same breath.

Finally, let the verse reshape prayer as alignment rather than negotiation. Over time, praying "according to his will" tends to change the one praying more than it changes the request.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does 1 John 5:14 mean God will give me anything I ask for?**
No. The verse explicitly conditions the promise on asking "according to his will." John is assuring believers that God genuinely hears and responds, not guaranteeing that every request will be granted as stated. A God who fulfilled every petition regardless of its wisdom would not be acting in love.

**How do I know if my prayer is according to God's will?**
Much of God's will is revealed in Scripture — holiness, forgiveness, wisdom, love for others — and those requests can be prayed with certainty. For matters Scripture does not address specifically, such as a particular outcome or healing, believers generally pray plainly and then submit, following Jesus' pattern in Luke 22:42.

**Why do some prayers go unanswered if God hears us?**
Scripture offers several considerations rather than one formula: a request may fall outside God's will, motives may be wrong (James 4:3), or the answer may be a purposeful no, as with Paul's thorn in 2 Corinthians 12:8–9. Christians differ on how to weigh these. What 1 John affirms is that unanswered does not mean unheard.

**What does "confidence" mean in this verse?**
The Greek word *parrēsia* described a free citizen's right to speak openly in a public assembly. Applied to prayer, it means believers approach God with freedom and boldness rather than as outsiders hoping for an audience. John uses the same word elsewhere in the letter for assurance before God.
