**Short answer:** Colossians 3:2 calls believers to fix their thoughts and affections on heavenly, eternal realities rather than on earthly, temporary ones. Because our life is now bound up with the risen Christ, our mindset and priorities should be oriented upward — shaped by where Christ is and what lasts forever.

## The context: raised with Christ

Colossians 3 begins with a foundation: "If then you were raised together with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated on the right hand of God" (3:1). Verse 2 restates and deepens that command. The reason follows in verses 3-4: "you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God. When Christ, our life, is revealed, then you will also be revealed with him in glory." Setting the mind above is not escapism; it flows from a new identity already secured in Christ.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The World English Bible reads: "Set your mind on the things that are above, not on the things that are on the earth."

- **"Set your mind"** — More than an occasional thought; it means to orient your whole way of thinking, your affections and priorities. Some translations render it "set your affection."
- **"on the things that are above"** — Christ and His kingdom, the eternal realities where He reigns (3:1). This is the true home and horizon of the believer.
- **"not on the things that are on the earth"** — Not a rejection of ordinary responsibilities, but a refusal to let earthly, passing things — status, possessions, sinful cravings (see 3:5) — govern the heart.

## How to apply it today

Colossians 3:2 is a compass for a distracted heart. It does not tell us to neglect work, family, or the world's real needs — Paul goes on to give very earthly instructions about anger, speech, and daily relationships (3:5-4:1). Rather, it asks: what governs your thinking and desires? When earthly ambitions, anxieties, or appetites dominate, this verse calls you to lift your gaze to Christ, letting eternal realities reorder your priorities. A mind set "above" handles earthly things more wisely, because it holds them in their proper, temporary place.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Colossians 3:2 mean I should ignore earthly responsibilities?**
No. The same chapter gives detailed instructions for earthly life — how to treat family, how to work, how to speak (3:5-4:1). Setting the mind "above" is about what ultimately governs your heart and priorities, not about neglecting God-given duties on earth. Heavenly-mindedness produces more faithful earthly living, not less.

**What are "the things that are above"?**
Verse 1 defines them: the realm "where Christ is, seated on the right hand of God." They are the eternal realities of Christ's kingdom — His rule, His glory, and the life we share with Him — as opposed to the passing values and desires of the fallen world.

**Is this the same as "be so heavenly minded you're no earthly good"?**
It is actually the opposite. Biblical heavenly-mindedness, as this passage shows, fuels holy, loving, and diligent earthly life. Christians who most clearly kept eternity in view have often been the most engaged in serving others here. A rightly ordered mind makes us more useful, not less.
