**Short answer:** Proverbs 19:21 means that while people make countless plans, it is God's purpose that ultimately stands. The verse is not against planning — it is a reality check that reminds us our plans are provisional and God's are certain, so we should hold ours loosely and align them with His.

## The context: wisdom about human limits

Proverbs is a collection of short sayings about living skillfully under God. A recurring theme in chapters 16 and 19 is the tension between human intention and divine sovereignty. Proverbs 16:9 says almost the same thing: "A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps" (KJV). These proverbs do not mock planning; they locate it within a bigger reality — God's overarching purpose.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: **"There are many devices in a man's heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand"** (Proverbs 19:21). The World English Bible renders it, "There are many plans in a man's heart, but Yahweh's counsel will prevail."

- **"many devices in a man's heart"** — "Devices" here means plans, schemes, intentions. The word *many* is telling: humans are endlessly generating ideas about how life should go.
- **"nevertheless"** — The pivot word. However full of plans we are, something outranks them.
- **"the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand"** — God's purpose is settled and unshakable. Where human plans are many and uncertain, God's counsel is singular and sure.

The proverb sets up a deliberate contrast: many plans versus one counsel; the heart's schemes versus the LORD's counsel; things that may or may not happen versus what "shall stand."

## Cross-references

- **Proverbs 16:9** — "A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps" (KJV).
- **Isaiah 46:10** — God declares, "My counsel shall stand, and I will do all my pleasure" (KJV).
- **James 4:13–15** — Say, "If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that" (KJV).
- **Psalm 33:11** — "The counsel of the LORD standeth for ever" (KJV).

## How to apply Proverbs 19:21 today

This verse is not a reason to stop planning — Scripture commends careful planning elsewhere (Proverbs 21:5). It is a reason to plan humbly. Make your plans, then hold them with open hands, willing for God to redirect them. When a plan falls apart, this proverb offers comfort: God's purpose was never dependent on your plan succeeding. A healthy rhythm is to plan seriously, pray sincerely, and submit the outcome to God.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Proverbs 19:21 mean planning is pointless?** No. Proverbs elsewhere praises diligent planning (see Proverbs 21:5). The verse teaches humility about our plans, not passivity — make plans, but recognize God's purpose has the final word.

**How is this verse comforting?** When your plans collapse, Proverbs 19:21 reminds you that God's good purpose does not collapse with them. His counsel stands regardless, so a failed plan is never the end of the story for those who trust Him.

**How is Proverbs 19:21 related to Proverbs 16:9?** They are near-twin sayings. Both contrast human planning with God's control — 16:9 says the LORD directs our steps, and 19:21 says His counsel prevails over our many plans. Together they frame a biblical view of guidance.
