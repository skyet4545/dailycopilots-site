**Short answer:** The Bible does not ignore depression or despair — it gives voice to it. Verses like Psalm 34:18, Psalm 42:11, Psalm 147:3, and Isaiah 41:10 show God drawing near to the brokenhearted, and the honest laments of Scripture make room for deep sadness while pointing toward hope.

*A note: Scripture is a source of comfort, not a substitute for medical care. If you are struggling with depression, please also reach out to a doctor, counselor, or a trusted person. If you are in crisis, contact a local emergency line or a suicide prevention hotline.*

## The context: the Bible takes sorrow seriously

One of the most comforting things about the Bible is how honestly it speaks about anguish. Whole psalms are laments. Elijah asked to die (1 Kings 19:4). The prophet Jeremiah is often called "the weeping prophet." Even Jesus was "deeply sorrowful" in Gethsemane (Matthew 26:38). Scripture never treats sadness as a lack of faith; it gives the sorrowful words to pray and a God who stays close.

## What the Bible says about depression and despair

**God is nearest to the crushed.** Psalm 34:18 (WEB): "Yahweh is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves those who have a crushed spirit." God does not withdraw from despair — He draws near to it.

**It's okay to question your own soul.** Psalm 42:11 shows the psalmist speaking honestly to his downcast heart: "Why are you in despair, my soul?... Hope in God!" The Bible models processing sadness out loud and choosing to hope anyway.

**God heals broken hearts.** Psalm 147:3 says He "heals the broken in heart, and binds up their wounds." Healing may be gradual, but the direction is toward wholeness.

**You are not alone in it.** Isaiah 41:10 promises God's presence and help: "Don't be afraid, for I am with you... I will strengthen you." Depression often lies that we are utterly alone; this verse counters that.

**Rest is offered to the weary.** Matthew 11:28: "Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest."

## Cross-references worth reading

- **Psalm 3:3** — God as "the lifter up of my head."
- **2 Corinthians 1:3–4** — "the God of all comfort."
- **Psalm 30:5** — "weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning."
- **Lamentations 3:22–23** — mercies "new every morning" amid grief.

## How to apply it today

If you are in a dark season, the Bible offers presence more than tidy answers. Praying the laments (like Psalm 42 or Psalm 88) can help you be honest with God rather than hiding. Verses like Psalm 34:18 remind you that God is *nearest* when you feel most crushed, not distant. Small rhythms — a single verse, an honest prayer, a step toward help — matter more than dramatic breakthroughs. And reaching out to others is deeply biblical; God often brings comfort through people (2 Corinthians 1:3–4). You do not have to carry this alone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is depression a sin according to the Bible?**
No. The Bible never treats sorrow or depression as sin. It records godly people in deep despair — Elijah, David, Jeremiah, even Jesus in Gethsemane — with compassion rather than blame. Scripture makes room for lament and points the sufferer toward God's nearness and help.

**What is a good Bible verse for someone who is depressed?**
Psalm 34:18 ("Yahweh is near to those who have a broken heart"), Psalm 147:3, and Matthew 11:28 are often shared for their comfort. Isaiah 41:10 reassures the fearful of God's presence. Choose one and return to it slowly rather than trying to absorb many at once.

**Does the Bible replace professional help for depression?**
No. Scripture offers real comfort and hope, but it is not a substitute for medical or professional care. God works through doctors, counselors, and caring people. If you are struggling, reaching out for help is wise and consistent with the Bible's picture of bearing one another's burdens (Galatians 6:2).
