**Short answer:** 1 Corinthians 13:4-7 defines real love by what it does, not by feeling — it is patient and kind, free of envy, pride, and self-seeking, and it bears, believes, hopes, and endures all things. Paul is describing the self-giving love (Greek *agapē*) God shows us and calls His people to show one another.

## The context: love over spiritual gifts

Though often read at weddings, 1 Corinthians 13 was not written about marriage. The Corinthian church prized flashy spiritual gifts and competed over status. Paul interrupts his teaching on gifts (chapters 12–14) to insist that without love, even the most impressive gifts are "nothing" (13:2). Verses 4-7 then define the love that must animate everything. Its first audience was a proud, divided congregation who needed to be told what love actually looks like in practice.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The World English Bible reads: "Love is patient and is kind. Love doesn't envy. Love doesn't brag, is not proud, doesn't behave itself inappropriately, doesn't seek its own way, is not provoked, takes no account of evil; doesn't rejoice in unrighteousness, but rejoices with the truth; bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things."

Notice most descriptions are verbs — love is active. Paul lists what love does and refuses to do:

- **Patient and kind** — the two positive foundations: it endures wrongs slowly and acts for others' good.
- **Doesn't envy, brag, or act proud** — love is not competitive or self-promoting; it is glad for others.
- **Doesn't behave inappropriately or seek its own way** — it is courteous and unselfish, putting others first.
- **Is not provoked; keeps no record of wrongs** — love is not easily angered and does not nurse grievances.
- **Rejoices with the truth, not in wrongdoing** — love delights in what is right.
- **Bears, believes, hopes, endures all things** — love covers, trusts, and perseveres to the end.

## Cross-references

- **John 13:34-35** — Jesus' new command to love as He loved.
- **Romans 5:8** — God demonstrated His love while we were still sinners.
- **1 John 4:8** — "God is love."
- **Galatians 5:22** — love as the first fruit of the Spirit.
- **Colossians 3:12-14** — put on patience, kindness, and love above all.

## How to apply it today

A helpful test is to read the passage substituting your own name: "I am patient, I am kind, I do not envy..." The gaps you feel show where love needs to grow. Because these are actions, love can be practiced even when feelings lag — choosing patience in traffic, kindness to a difficult coworker, or refusing to keep score in a marriage. Ultimately the only person of whom every line is fully true is Jesus, so the passage both convicts us and points us to the One whose love supplies what we lack.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is 1 Corinthians 13 about romantic love?**
Not originally. Paul wrote it to a church struggling with pride and division, describing self-giving love (*agapē*) among believers. It applies beautifully to marriage — which is why it is a popular wedding reading — but its scope is far wider than romance.

**What does "keeps no record of wrongs" mean practically?**
It means love does not store up grievances to use later or hold past offenses over someone's head. This does not erase the need for justice or accountability, but it refuses bitterness and chooses forgiveness. It reflects how God, in Christ, does not count our sins against us (2 Corinthians 5:19).

**How can love "believe all things" without being naive?**
It means love is inclined to trust and think the best rather than assume the worst. It is not gullibility that ignores clear evidence, but a generous posture that gives people the benefit of the doubt. Paired with "rejoices with the truth," it seeks reality while refusing cynicism.
