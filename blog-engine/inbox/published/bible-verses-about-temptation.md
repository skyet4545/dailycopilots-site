**Short answer:** The Bible treats temptation as a real and common experience — not sin itself, but the pull toward it — and offers hope: God is faithful, always provides a way of escape, and gives strength to resist. Scripture teaches that temptation comes from within (our desires) and from the enemy, never from God, and that Jesus both understands our temptations and helps us through them.

## What the Bible teaches about temptation

**Temptation is common, and God provides a way out.**
- 1 Corinthians 10:13 — "There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape."

**God does not tempt us; temptation grows from within.**
- James 1:13–14 — "Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God... But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust."

**Stay alert and pray.**
- Matthew 26:41 — "Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak."
- Luke 22:40 — "Pray that ye enter not into temptation."

**Resist, and flee.**
- James 4:7 — "Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you."
- 1 Peter 5:8–9 — be sober and vigilant; "whom resist stedfast in the faith."
- 1 Corinthians 6:18 and 2 Timothy 2:22 — Scripture sometimes counsels fleeing rather than fighting.

## Jesus understands and helps

A great comfort in Scripture is that Jesus faced temptation Himself.
- Hebrews 4:15 — He "was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin."
- Hebrews 2:18 — "In that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted."
- Matthew 4:1–11 — Jesus answered each temptation in the wilderness with Scripture ("It is written").

Because Jesus was tempted and did not sin, He both sympathizes with us and provides real help.

## How to apply it today

Scripture offers a practical pattern. First, expect temptation without shame — being tempted is not sinning. Second, watch and pray before pressure hits, and stock your heart with Scripture, as Jesus did. Third, look for the "way of escape" God promises; often it's as simple as leaving the situation. Fourth, resist in dependence on God, not willpower alone. And when you fail, remember that Jesus sympathizes and that confession restores (1 John 1:9). The consistent message is hope: no temptation is beyond God's power to help you through.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is being tempted a sin?**
No. Scripture distinguishes temptation from sin — even Jesus "was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15). Temptation becomes sin when we yield to it, but experiencing the pull itself is not sin.

**Does God tempt people?**
No. James 1:13 states plainly that God tempts no one; temptation arises from our own desires and from the enemy. God does allow testing that can strengthen faith, but He is never the source of enticement to sin.

**What is the "way of escape" in 1 Corinthians 10:13?**
It's God's promise that with every temptation He provides a means to endure or get out, so no temptation is beyond bearing. Practically, this can be a change of circumstances, the strength to resist, prayer, or simply removing yourself from the situation.
