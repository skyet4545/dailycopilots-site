**Short answer:** The Bible presents kindness first as something God *is* and does toward undeserving people, and then as something His people extend to others because they've received it. The clearest statement is Ephesians 4:32: "And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you" (KJV) — the model and the motive in one sentence.

## Where kindness sits in Scripture

Biblical kindness is not merely niceness. In the Old Testament it overlaps with the rich Hebrew word *chesed* — loyal, covenant love that keeps showing up. In the New Testament, the Greek *chrēstotēs* describes a settled disposition of active goodwill.

That means Scripture's kindness has a spine. It is not conflict-avoidance or flattery. It can coexist with honest correction — Proverbs says faithful are the wounds of a friend (27:6) — and it targets people who cannot repay.

## God's kindness comes first

Every command to be kind rests on God's kindness having come first.

- **Psalm 145:9** — "The LORD is good to all: and his tender mercies are over all his works" (KJV). God's kindness is universal in scope, not reserved for the deserving.
- **Luke 6:35** — Jesus tells His followers to love enemies and do good, "for he is kind unto the unthankful and to the evil" (KJV). This is the sharpest definition of divine kindness in the Gospels.
- **Romans 2:4** — "the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance" (KJV). Kindness is not God's alternative to dealing with sin; it's His method of drawing people out of it.
- **Titus 3:4–5** — the kindness and love of God our Savior appeared, and He saved us not by works of righteousness but according to His mercy.
- **Lamentations 3:22–23** — His compassions fail not; they are new every morning.

## Kindness as the character of God's people

- **Ephesians 4:32** — be kind, tenderhearted, forgiving, "even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you" (KJV). Note the standard: not as much as others deserve, but as much as you've received.
- **Colossians 3:12** — "Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering" (KJV). Kindness is something you *put on*, like clothing — a deliberate act, not a temperament you either have or don't.
- **Galatians 5:22–23** — kindness appears in the fruit of the Spirit (the KJV renders the term "gentleness"). It is grown by the Spirit, not manufactured by effort alone.
- **1 Corinthians 13:4** — "Charity suffereth long, and is kind" (KJV). In Paul's definition of love, kindness is the second thing named.
- **Micah 6:8** — what the LORD requires: to do justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God.

## Kindness toward the poor and the outsider

Scripture consistently aims kindness at those with no leverage.

- **Proverbs 19:17** — "He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the LORD" (KJV). Kindness to the poor is treated as a transaction with God Himself.
- **Proverbs 14:31** — oppressing the poor reproaches his Maker; honoring Him means having mercy on the needy.
- **Zechariah 7:9–10** — God commands executing true judgment, showing mercy and compassion, and not oppressing the widow, orphan, foreigner, or poor.
- **Luke 10:30–37** — the Good Samaritan, where kindness crosses ethnic and religious lines and costs money, time, and safety.
- **Matthew 25:35–40** — food, drink, welcome, clothing, and visitation given to "the least of these" are received by Christ as done to Him.

## Kindness that costs something

- **Proverbs 11:17** — "The merciful man doeth good to his own soul" (KJV). Kindness shapes the one who gives it.
- **Romans 12:20** — if your enemy is hungry, feed him. Kindness is a Christian's response to hostility.
- **2 Samuel 9:1–7** — David seeks out Mephibosheth, Jonathan's disabled son, to show him kindness for Jonathan's sake, restoring his land and a permanent seat at the king's table. It's one of Scripture's clearest narrative pictures of undeserved kindness.
- **Ruth 2:8–16** — Boaz's deliberate provision for Ruth, a foreign widow, going beyond what the law required.

## How to live it out

Start where Ephesians 4:32 starts: with what you've received. Kindness that isn't fed by an awareness of God's kindness toward you tends to become either performance or resentment.

Then make it concrete. Colossians 3:12's "put on" language suggests a decision made before feelings arrive — choosing the generous interpretation of someone's motives, doing the small unasked thing, speaking the encouraging word you thought but didn't say. Scripture's examples are specific and often material: bread, clothing, shelter, a seat at the table.

Aim it where there's no return. Luke 6:35 and Luke 14:12–14 both press toward those who cannot repay. And keep it honest: biblical kindness includes truthful words (Ephesians 4:15) and doesn't require pretending harm didn't happen. Faithful Christians differ on how kindness and firm boundaries fit together in specific relationships — Scripture commends both patient forbearance and wise caution (Proverbs 22:3), and wisdom is required to apply them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is kindness a fruit of the Spirit?**
Yes. Galatians 5:22–23 lists it among the fruit of the Spirit — the KJV translates the Greek term as "gentleness," while most modern versions render it "kindness." Either way, Scripture presents it as something the Spirit grows in a believer rather than a personality trait some people simply lack.

**What's the difference between kindness and being nice?**
Niceness aims at pleasantness; biblical kindness aims at the other person's good, which sometimes requires honesty or cost. Proverbs 27:6 says a friend's wounds are faithful, and Ephesians 4:15 pairs truth with love. Kindness in Scripture also reaches enemies and the poor — people niceness has no particular reason to seek out.

**What is the main Bible verse about kindness?**
Ephesians 4:32 is the most quoted: "And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you" (KJV). Colossians 3:12 and Galatians 5:22 are close companions, and Luke 6:35 gives the underlying reason — God is kind to the unthankful and evil.

**Does the Bible say to be kind to enemies?**
Yes, explicitly. Luke 6:35 commands loving enemies and doing good to them, and Romans 12:20 instructs feeding a hungry enemy. Jesus grounds this in God's own conduct, who sends sun and rain on the just and unjust alike (Matthew 5:45).
