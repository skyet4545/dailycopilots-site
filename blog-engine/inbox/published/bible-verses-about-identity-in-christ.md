**Short answer:** The Bible teaches that a believer's deepest identity is defined by their relationship to Jesus, not by achievements, failures, or others' opinions. In Christ, Scripture says a person is a new creation, a beloved child of God, chosen, forgiven, and secure. This God-given identity is a gift received by grace, not something earned.

## What the Bible teaches about identity in Christ

**You are a new creation.**
- 2 Corinthians 5:17 — "If any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new."

**You are a child of God.**
- John 1:12 — to those who receive Christ, He gives "power to become the sons of God."
- Romans 8:15–16 — believers receive "the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father."
- 1 John 3:1 — "Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called the sons of God."

**You are chosen and loved.**
- Ephesians 1:4–5 — God "hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world," predestining us "unto the adoption of children."
- 1 Peter 2:9 — "a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people."

**You are forgiven and free from condemnation.**
- Romans 8:1 — "There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus."
- Colossians 2:13–14 — God forgave our trespasses, canceling the record against us.

**Your life is hidden and secure in Christ.**
- Galatians 2:20 — "I am crucified with Christ... Christ liveth in me."
- Colossians 3:3 — "your life is hid with Christ in God."

## A workmanship with purpose

Identity in Christ isn't only about status; it comes with purpose. Ephesians 2:10 says believers are God's "workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works." Who you are in Christ leads naturally to what you're made to do — not to earn love, but as a response to it.

## Cross-references

- Romans 8:37 — "more than conquerors through him that loved us."
- 2 Corinthians 5:21 — made "the righteousness of God in him."
- Galatians 3:26 — "children of God by faith in Christ Jesus."

## How to apply it today

When identity is built on performance, approval, or comparison, it rises and falls with circumstances. Scripture offers a steadier foundation: who God says you are in Christ doesn't change with your best or worst day. A practical habit is to answer the lie of shame or self-doubt with a specific truth — "new creation," "child of God," "no condemnation" — and let Scripture, not your feelings, have the final word. Because this identity is received by grace, it can be rested in rather than achieved.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does "identity in Christ" mean?**
It means that a believer's core sense of who they are is grounded in their relationship with Jesus — as a new creation, child of God, chosen and forgiven — rather than in accomplishments or others' opinions. Scripture says this identity is given by grace (Ephesians 2:8–9), not earned.

**What does it mean to be a "new creation" in 2 Corinthians 5:17?**
It means that in Christ a person is spiritually made new — the old life passes away and a new one begins. It points to a transformed standing before God and the start of an ongoing renewal, not merely improved behavior.

**Does my identity in Christ depend on my performance?**
No. Scripture presents this identity as God's gift by grace through faith (Ephesians 2:8–9), and says believers face "no condemnation" in Christ (Romans 8:1). Because it rests on God's work rather than ours, it remains secure even on our worst days.
