**Short answer:** Isaiah 30:21 pictures God guiding His people so closely that they hear a voice behind them saying, "This is the way, walk in it," whenever they start to drift left or right. It is a promise of clear, corrective guidance to a people who return to trusting Him.

## The context: a warning that turns to hope

Isaiah 30 opens as a rebuke. Judah was tempted to seek security from Egypt instead of from God (vv. 1–2). But the chapter turns toward mercy: "the LORD will be gracious unto thee" (v. 18, KJV). Verses 20–21 describe what life looks like when the people finally listen to God rather than to political fear — their Teacher will no longer be hidden, and they will hear His direction. Verse 21 is that promise of guidance.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: **"And thine ears shall hear a word behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it, when ye turn to the right hand, and when ye turn to the left"** (Isaiah 30:21).

- **"thine ears shall hear a word behind thee"** — The voice comes from *behind*, the image of a shepherd or guide walking behind the flock, watching and directing. God's guidance follows you attentively.
- **"This is the way, walk ye in it"** — Clear, personal instruction. Not vague feelings but direction for actual steps.
- **"when ye turn to the right hand, and when ye turn to the left"** — The correction comes precisely when you begin to stray. God does not only affirm the right path; He redirects wandering.

The promise assumes a relationship of listening. Verse 21 follows verse 20's picture of the people finally seeing their Teacher — guidance flows to those willing to receive it.

## How to apply Isaiah 30:21 today

Guidance in this verse is tied to nearness. The people hear the voice because they have turned back to God as their Teacher. Practically, that means staying close through Scripture, prayer, and a willingness to obey. The "voice behind you" is not usually audible; more often it is God's Word and the prompting of His Spirit gently correcting your course. When you sense you are drifting, this verse invites you to slow down and listen for the redirection rather than pushing ahead on your own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Isaiah 30:21 promise an audible voice from God?** The verse uses the image of hearing a voice, but its point is clear, personal guidance — not necessarily an audible sound. For believers today, that guidance typically comes through Scripture, prayer, and the Holy Spirit's leading.

**Why does the voice come from "behind"?** It pictures a guide or shepherd walking behind the flock, watching the whole path and directing from a vantage the sheep do not have. It suggests attentive, protective guidance rather than distant instruction.

**What is the condition for this promise?** The surrounding verses tie it to returning to God as Teacher and refusing false sources of security (Isaiah 30:15–20). Guidance is promised to those who listen and trust, not to those who ignore Him.
