**Short answer:** John 3:16 means that God's love for the world moved Him to give His Son, Jesus, so that anyone who believes in Him will not perish but have eternal life. It compresses the heart of the Christian message into one sentence: God's love, His costly gift, the response of faith, and the promise of everlasting life.

## The context

John 3:16 comes from a nighttime conversation between Jesus and Nicodemus, a Pharisee and Jewish leader (John 3:1–21). Nicodemus comes with questions, and Jesus speaks of being "born again" and being lifted up "as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness" (John 3:14) — a reference to His coming death. Verse 16 explains the reason behind it all: God's love.

Whether verse 16 is Jesus' own words continuing or the narrator John's reflection is debated, but the meaning is unaffected.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life."

- **"For God so loved the world"** — the motive is love, directed at "the world," the whole of humanity in its need. "So" points to the manner and extent of that love.
- **"That he gave his only begotten Son"** — love expressed in a costly gift. "Only begotten" (Greek *monogenēs*) means unique, one-of-a-kind. The giving points toward the cross.
- **"That whosoever believeth in him"** — the response is faith, and it's open to anyone. "Whosoever" is deliberately wide.
- **"Should not perish"** — the alternative that faith rescues from; a real peril of being lost.
- **"But have everlasting life"** — the promise. In John, "eternal life" is both a future hope and a present relationship with God that begins now (John 17:3).

## Cross-references

- John 3:17 — God sent the Son "not to condemn the world, but that the world through him might be saved."
- Romans 5:8 — "God commendeth his love toward us, in that... Christ died for us."
- 1 John 4:9–10 — God sent His Son as the demonstration of love.
- John 17:3 — eternal life is knowing God and Jesus Christ.
- Ephesians 2:8–9 — salvation by grace through faith.

## How to apply it today

Familiarity can dull this verse, but slowing down over it renews its weight. It says the initiative is God's — He loved, He gave — before anyone earns it. It says the gift cost God His Son. And it says the door is wide: "whosoever."

For the believer, it's an anchor of assurance and gratitude. For anyone exploring faith, it's an invitation: the response asked is to believe, to trust in Christ. Read it personally — God so loved *the world*, which includes you — and let it move from a memorized line to a truth you rest in.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does "only begotten Son" mean?**
The Greek word *monogenēs* means unique or one-of-a-kind, emphasizing that Jesus is God's one and only Son. It underscores the costliness of the gift — God gave not just anything, but His unique Son.

**Who does "the world" refer to in John 3:16?**
"The world" refers broadly to humanity in its need and lostness. The verse stresses the wide scope of God's love, reinforced by the word "whosoever," which opens the offer of eternal life to anyone who believes.

**Does John 3:16 mean everyone is automatically saved?**
No. The promise is specifically for "whosoever believeth in him." God's love and gift are offered to all, but the verse ties not perishing and eternal life to the response of faith in Jesus.
