If you want Scripture to help with anxiety, start with **YouVersion** — completely free, ad-free, and its reading-plan catalog includes dozens of short plans written specifically for anxiety, fear, and worry. If you need something to listen to when your mind will not slow down at night, **Abide** is the strongest pick: guided, Scripture-anchored meditations and sleep stories built around exactly that problem. **Soultime** fits better if you want anxiety tracked over time rather than soothed in the moment; **Dwell** is the choice if hearing Scripture read aloud is what calms you. **Hallow** is the deepest option for Catholic users, and **Glorify** bundles devotional, worship, and sleep content into one daily rhythm.

One caveat up front: none of these is a calming app *and* a study app. If your anxiety comes with questions — *does this verse actually mean God will fix this?* — a study tool like **Bible Copilot** answers a different need. Pick based on which problem you actually have.

## YouVersion — start here, because it is free

There is no reason to pay for an anxiety app before trying this one. YouVersion carries 3,500+ Bible versions across 2,300+ languages, offers audio, and does not run ads, sell your data, or gate the text behind a subscription.

It belongs at the top of an anxiety list because of the plan catalog, which specifically covers anxiety, fear, grief, and stress — usually as three-to-seven-day plans with a short passage and a paragraph of reflection per day. That format matters more than it sounds: when you are anxious, a five-minute structured reading is far more likely to get done than an open Bible and no plan.

What it does not do is explain anything in depth. It hands you the passage and a devotional thought, and stops there.

## Abide — the best pick for anxious nights

Abide is meditation-first: guided audio built on a Scripture passage, with a large share of the catalog aimed directly at anxiety, stress, worry, and sleep. The sleep stories are what people stay for — long, slow, Scripture-based audio designed to be fallen asleep to rather than finished.

It is free to download, with a daily meditation and some sleep content at no cost; the full library sits behind a subscription Abide lists at $9.99/month or $39.99/year, after a free trial. Notably, Abide states that reduced rates are available on request and that it gives one-year free subscriptions to people who cannot afford one — worth knowing if cost is the blocker.

The trade-off: Abide is audio. If you want to read, study, or search, this is not that app.

## Soultime — if you want to see the pattern, not just the moment

Soultime treats anxiety as recurring rather than as a single bad evening. It carries 200+ guided sessions organized by emotion, plus a mood tracker, a journal, sleep stories, and an AI-narrated audio Bible. The mood tracker can notify people you nominate when you are having a hard stretch — a feature no other app here offers.

Pricing is a 7-day trial, then plans from around $7.99/month or $59.99/year. It is endorsed by the former Archbishop of Canterbury and has been recommended by the Church in Wales for clergy wellbeing — a fair signal of how seriously it takes the mental-health side.

## Dwell — if hearing it is what helps

Some people calm down by reading; others need Scripture spoken over them. Dwell is an audio Bible with a choice of narrator voices, music beds, and structured listening plans, and it is unusually well made. Pricing is a 7-day trial, then Solo at $59.99/year, Duo at $89.99, or Family at $109.99 for up to six. It is the most expensive per-feature option here and it does one thing — but it does that one thing better than the general apps.

## Hallow — the Catholic option

Hallow is the largest Catholic prayer app, and its free tier is genuinely usable: hundreds of sessions including contemplative prayer, the rosary, the daily examen, and Scripture-based sessions on anxiety. Premium runs $9.99/month or $69.99/year, with a Friends & Family annual plan at $119.99 covering up to six, all starting with a free trial. If you are not Catholic much of the framing will feel unfamiliar; if you are, nothing else here is built for you the way this is.

## Glorify — one app, one daily rhythm

Glorify bundles a daily devotional, worship music, sleep audio, and a journal into a single non-denominational flow, and its free tier is generous enough to try the rhythm for a few weeks before deciding. Its annual price has been reported at different figures across regions and promotions, so check what the app actually shows you rather than any third-party number — including this one.

## Bible Copilot — when the anxiety comes with questions

Meditation apps calm you; they do not explain the text. If your anxiety is tangled up with *what does this passage actually mean* — does "in nothing be anxious" mean anxiety is a sin? does 1 Peter 5:7 promise the circumstance changes? — you need study, not audio.

Bible Copilot works through six guided modes — Summary, Observe, Interpret, Theology, Apply, Apologetics — following the inductive order of observation before interpretation before application, and every answer cites the Scripture behind it. It is [free on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6758913373) with three questions per day and no account required; Pro is $9.99/month or $49.99/year with a 7-day trial. iPhone and iPad only, and it is not a calming app — no meditations, no sleep audio, no worship music.

## What Scripture says, and what an app cannot do

Philippians 4:6 (WEB) reads: "In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God." And 1 Peter 5:7: "casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you."

Christians differ, in good faith, on how to hold those verses alongside clinical anxiety. Many pastors and Christian counselors read them as an invitation rather than an accusation — a command to bring worry somewhere, not a verdict that having it is failure. If a verse is landing on you as condemnation, that is a reason to talk to someone rather than read it alone at 2 a.m.

**These apps are not treatment.** They can genuinely help with everyday worry and the spiritual side of anxiety, but none substitutes for a doctor, a licensed therapist, or medication when those are what the situation calls for. If anxiety is interfering with sleep, work, or relationships, please talk to a professional.

Most people end up with two apps: one for calm, one for study. No one has built a good version of both in the same product yet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best free Bible app for anxiety?**
YouVersion, without much competition — entirely free, no ads, no premium tier, and a plan catalog with many short plans built around anxiety, fear, and worry. Abide, Hallow, and Glorify have free tiers worth using too, but each keeps most of its library behind a subscription.

**Is there a Bible app that reads Scripture to me when I cannot sleep?**
Yes — that is Abide's core strength, and its sleep stories are designed to be fallen asleep to rather than completed. Dwell is better if you want the biblical text read continuously rather than a meditation built around it. YouVersion's free audio Bibles are the no-cost way to test whether listening helps before you subscribe to anything.

**Do Christian meditation apps conflict with the Bible?**
The apps here all practice Scripture-anchored meditation — dwelling on a specific passage rather than emptying the mind — which has direct biblical precedent in Psalm 1 and Joshua 1:8. Christians hold different views on the broader category, and it is a fair question for your own pastor. If you are unsure, start with YouVersion's reading plans, which involve no meditation practice at all.

**Can a Bible app replace therapy or medication for anxiety?**
No. These apps address the spiritual and devotional side of anxiety, and several are good at it, but none is clinical treatment. If anxiety is affecting your sleep, work, or relationships, see a doctor or licensed counselor — many Christian counselors work alongside these practices rather than against them.

**Which app helps me understand anxiety verses instead of just reading them?**
That is a study question, not a meditation one. Bible Copilot is built for it: Observe, Interpret, and Apply modes walk a passage in that order and cite the Scripture behind each answer. Blue Letter Bible is the strongest free alternative if you want to dig into the Greek or Hebrew yourself, though it expects you to do the interpreting.
