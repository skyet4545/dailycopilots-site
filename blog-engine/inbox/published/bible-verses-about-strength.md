**Short answer:** The Bible's verses about strength consistently locate real strength not in ourselves but in God. Scripture promises that God gives power to the weary, that His strength is made perfect in our weakness, and that those who wait on the Lord renew their strength. The recurring message: human strength runs out, but God's does not, and He shares it with those who rely on Him.

## What the Bible means by "strength"

In Scripture, strength is rarely about self-confidence or raw willpower. It is most often about depending on God as the true source of endurance, courage, and inner steadiness. The Bible is honest that people grow tired, afraid, and weak — and it repeatedly points beyond human resources to God Himself as "strength." Understanding this reframes these verses: they are not slogans for self-reliance but invitations to lean on God.

## Strength that comes from waiting on God

Some of the most beloved strength verses tie strength to trust and dependence:

- **Isaiah 40:31** — "those who wait for the Lord will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles." Strength here is renewed, not self-generated.
- **Psalm 46:1** — "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble."
- **Psalm 28:7** — "The Lord is my strength and my shield. My heart has trusted in him, and I am helped."
- **Nehemiah 8:10** — "the joy of the Lord is your strength."

## Strength in weakness

The New Testament deepens the theme, teaching that God's power is most clearly displayed when we are weak:

- **2 Corinthians 12:9** — God told Paul, "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness."
- **Philippians 4:13** — "I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me." In context, Paul means he can face any circumstance — plenty or need — through Christ, not that he can achieve anything he wants.
- **Isaiah 41:10** — "Don't be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you. Yes, I will help you."

## Strength to be courageous

Scripture also connects strength to courage grounded in God's presence:

- **Joshua 1:9** — "Be strong and courageous... for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go."
- **Ephesians 6:10** — "be strong in the Lord, and in the strength of his might."
- **Psalm 27:1** — "The Lord is the strength of my life. Of whom shall I be afraid?"

## How to apply these verses today

When you feel depleted, resist the temptation to simply try harder. These verses point a different direction: bring your weakness honestly to God and ask Him for strength you do not have. Practically, that means prayer before striving, feeding on Scripture that reminds you where power comes from, and reinterpreting your weak moments as the very place God's strength shows up (2 Corinthians 12:9). Waiting on God (Isaiah 40:31) is not passivity but active, trusting dependence — the posture in which strength is renewed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the most well-known Bible verse about strength?**
Philippians 4:13 — "I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me" — is among the most quoted. Read in context, it is Paul's testimony that Christ gives him strength to face any circumstance, whether abundance or need, rather than a promise of unlimited personal achievement.

**Does the Bible say God gives strength to the weak?**
Yes, clearly. Isaiah 40:29 says God "gives power to the weak," and 2 Corinthians 12:9 teaches that God's power "is made perfect in weakness." Scripture repeatedly presents weakness as the setting where God's strength becomes most evident.

**How do I find strength from God when I feel exhausted?**
Scripture points to waiting on the Lord (Isaiah 40:31), casting your cares on Him (1 Peter 5:7), and drawing on the joy of the Lord as your strength (Nehemiah 8:10). Practically, this means honest prayer, resting in God's presence, and steeping your mind in His promises rather than relying solely on your own reserves.
