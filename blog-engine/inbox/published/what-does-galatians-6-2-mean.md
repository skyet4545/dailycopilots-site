**Short answer:** Galatians 6:2 means that Christians are called to help carry each other's heavy loads — the sins, sorrows, and struggles that weigh a person down — and that doing so is how we "fulfill the law of Christ," the command to love one another. Real Christian community shares burdens rather than leaving people to carry them alone.

## The context: restoring the fallen

Paul writes Galatians to churches tempted to swap grace for rule-keeping. In chapter 6 he turns to how a grace-shaped community treats its weak members. Verse 1 says that if someone is "overtaken in a fault," the spiritual should restore that person gently. Verse 2 flows directly from this: restoring the fallen means coming underneath their burden and helping lift it. The verse is not an abstract slogan — it is instruction for a real church learning to carry one another instead of judging one another.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: **"Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ"** (Galatians 6:2).

- **"Bear ye one another's burdens"** — The word for burden here pictures a crushing weight, something too heavy for one person. To "bear" it is to get under it with someone, sharing the load.
- **"one another's"** — This is mutual. Everyone both carries and is carried; no one is only the helper or only the helped.
- **"and so fulfil the law of Christ"** — The "law of Christ" is love (see John 13:34; Galatians 5:14). Burden-bearing is not an add-on to the Christian life; it is love made practical.

Some readers notice that verse 5 says "every man shall bear his own burden." The tension is resolved by the different words: verse 2 speaks of crushing loads we share, verse 5 of the personal responsibility each person carries before God. Both are true.

## Cross-references

- **John 13:34** — Jesus' new command: "love one another; as I have loved you" (KJV).
- **Romans 15:1** — The strong ought "to bear the infirmities of the weak" (KJV).
- **1 Corinthians 12:26** — When one member suffers, "all the members suffer with it" (KJV).
- **Galatians 5:14** — The whole law is fulfilled in "love thy neighbour as thyself" (KJV).

## How to apply Galatians 6:2 today

This verse turns love from a feeling into an action. Look for the person near you carrying something heavy — grief, financial strain, a besetting sin, exhaustion — and get underneath it with them. That may mean a meal, a listening ear, practical help, or gentle accountability. It also means letting others carry your burdens; refusing help can be its own kind of pride. A church that lives this verse becomes a place where no one is crushed alone, and in that shared load the command of Christ is quietly fulfilled.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the "law of Christ" in Galatians 6:2?** It is the command to love one another as Christ loved us (John 13:34). Paul says elsewhere that love fulfills the whole law (Galatians 5:14), so bearing burdens is love put into practice.

**Doesn't Galatians 6:5 contradict this by saying we each carry our own burden?** No — the two verses use different words. Verse 2 speaks of crushing loads meant to be shared; verse 5 speaks of the personal accountability each of us has before God. They complement rather than contradict.

**Who are we supposed to help?** The immediate context (verse 1) is restoring a fellow believer who has stumbled, but the principle extends broadly to anyone weighed down. Christians are called to notice and share the heavy loads of the people around them.
