**Short answer:** The Bible does not treat all anger as sin, but it warns repeatedly that anger is dangerous, quick to lead us astray, and must be handled with self-control. Scripture calls us to be "slow to anger," to not let anger fester, and to leave vengeance to God, while allowing that anger against genuine evil can be righteous when it is free of sin.

## Is all anger a sin?

Scripture distinguishes between anger itself and what we do with it. God Himself is described as angry at injustice and sin, and Jesus showed anger at hard-heartedness (Mark 3:5). So anger is not automatically sinful. But human anger easily turns selfish, cruel, and destructive, which is why the Bible surrounds it with so many warnings. The key issue is control and cause: what are we angry about, and what does our anger produce?

## Verses on being slow to anger

- **James 1:19–20** — "let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God" (KJV).
- **Proverbs 15:1** — "A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger" (KJV).
- **Proverbs 14:29** — "He that is slow to wrath is of great understanding" (KJV).
- **Proverbs 16:32** — "he that is slow to anger is better than the mighty" (KJV).
- **Ecclesiastes 7:9** — "Be not hasty in thy spirit to be angry: for anger resteth in the bosom of fools" (KJV).

## Verses on not letting anger control you

- **Ephesians 4:26–27** — "Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: Neither give place to the devil" (KJV). Anger is allowed, but it must not be nursed or acted on sinfully.
- **Ephesians 4:31** — "Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger... be put away from you" (KJV).
- **Colossians 3:8** — "put off all these; anger, wrath, malice" (KJV).
- **Proverbs 29:11** — "A fool uttereth all his mind: but a wise man keepeth it in till afterwards" (KJV).

## Verses on leaving vengeance to God

- **Romans 12:19** — "avenge not yourselves... Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord" (KJV).
- **Proverbs 20:22** — "Say not thou, I will recompense evil; but wait on the LORD" (KJV).

## How to apply these verses today

Scripture's counsel on anger is practical. Be "swift to hear" before reacting (James 1:19). Deal with anger quickly rather than letting it harden into bitterness overnight (Ephesians 4:26). Answer gently instead of escalating (Proverbs 15:1). And when you have been wronged, resist the urge to repay evil, trusting God to handle justice (Romans 12:19). Where anger burns against real injustice, let it move you toward good rather than sin. The goal is not to feel nothing, but to be, like God, "slow to anger" and quick to mercy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the Bible say anger is always a sin?** No. God is described as angry at sin, and Jesus displayed anger at hard-heartedness (Mark 3:5). Ephesians 4:26 even says "be angry, and sin not." The Bible treats anger as dangerous and easily corrupted, but not sinful in itself — the issue is its cause and what it produces.

**What does "do not let the sun go down on your anger" mean?** Ephesians 4:26 urges believers not to let anger linger and harden into bitterness. Deal with it promptly — through forgiveness, honest conversation, or prayer — rather than nursing a grudge that gives the devil a foothold (v. 27).

**How does the Bible say I should handle anger?** Be slow to anger and quick to listen (James 1:19), answer gently (Proverbs 15:1), refuse to take revenge (Romans 12:19), and put away bitterness (Ephesians 4:31). The consistent theme is self-control and trusting God with justice rather than acting out of rage.
