**Short answer:** Proverbs 28:13 means that covering up sin — hiding it, denying it, excusing it — leads to failure and ruin, while honestly confessing and turning away from sin opens the door to mercy. The verse ties two actions together: real confession and genuine forsaking. Both are needed to find compassion.

## The context

Proverbs 28 belongs to a section of the book (chapters 25–29) collected by "the men of Hezekiah" (Proverbs 25:1). Many proverbs here contrast the righteous and the wicked, and several deal with rulers, justice, and integrity. Verse 13 addresses something universal: what to do with our own wrongdoing.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: "He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy."

- **"He that covereth his sins"** — the person who conceals wrongdoing, whether from others, from God, or from himself. This is the instinct to hide, minimize, or shift blame.
- **"Shall not prosper"** — will not succeed or flourish. Hidden sin festers; it doesn't disappear.
- **"But whoso confesseth"** — the one who openly acknowledges the sin as sin, without excuses.
- **"And forsaketh them"** — and abandons it, turning away rather than merely admitting it. Confession without change is incomplete.
- **"Shall have mercy"** — will find compassion. The promise is mercy, not condemnation, for the honest and repentant.

The contrast is striking: covering leads nowhere good; confessing-and-forsaking leads to mercy. Concealment and honesty produce opposite outcomes.

## Cross-references

- Psalm 32:3–5 — David describes the misery of silence, then relief when he confessed.
- Psalm 51 — David's prayer of confession after his sin.
- 1 John 1:9 — "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us."
- Luke 15:17–24 — the prodigal son confesses and is met with mercy.
- James 5:16 — "Confess your faults one to another."

## How to apply it today

This verse names a temptation everyone knows: the pull to hide failures rather than face them. It promises that honesty is the path to freedom, not further loss.

Practically, that means bringing sin into the light — first to God in prayer, and where appropriate to trusted people or those you've wronged. But note both halves. Confessing while clinging to the sin isn't what the verse describes; "forsaketh" calls for a genuine turn. The pattern is honest admission plus real change, and the result promised is mercy — a hope that draws people toward confession rather than away from it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between confessing and forsaking sin?**
Confessing is honestly acknowledging the wrong as sin; forsaking is turning away from it and changing course. Proverbs 28:13 requires both — admission without abandonment is incomplete, and both together open the way to mercy.

**Why does hiding sin lead to failure?**
Concealed sin doesn't resolve; it festers and grows, often causing inner turmoil and further harm, as David describes in Psalm 32:3–4. The verse observes that cover-ups don't "prosper" because they never actually deal with the problem.

**Does Proverbs 28:13 connect to the New Testament?**
Yes. Its promise of mercy for confession echoes strongly in 1 John 1:9, which says God is faithful to forgive confessed sin, and in Jesus' parable of the prodigal son (Luke 15), who confesses and is welcomed with compassion.
