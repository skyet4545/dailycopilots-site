**Short answer:** Philippians 2:3 means Christians should reject selfish ambition and empty pride, and instead cultivate genuine humility that counts other people as more important than themselves. It is a call to put others' interests ahead of our own — the same mindset Jesus displayed.

## The context: unity through humility

Paul writes to a beloved church at Philippi that faced pressure from outside and friction inside. In chapter 2 he pleads for unity, and he locates the secret of unity not in agreement about everything but in humility. Verses 3–4 spell out the attitude; verses 5–11 give the supreme example — Christ, who emptied Himself, took the form of a servant, and was obedient to death. Philippians 2:3 is the doorway into that famous passage about the mind of Christ.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: **"Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves"** (Philippians 2:3).

- **"strife"** — selfish ambition or rivalry; doing things to win or to be seen as superior.
- **"vainglory"** — empty conceit, craving hollow praise. Paul rules out both the competitive and the self-glorifying motive.
- **"lowliness of mind"** — humility. In the ancient world this was often a term of contempt; the New Testament makes it a virtue.
- **"esteem other better than themselves"** — to reckon, or count, others as more important. This is a deliberate judgment, not a feeling of low self-worth. It means treating others' needs and interests as genuinely mattering more than your own comfort.

Note what the verse does not say. It does not command you to believe you are worthless or that everyone is more talented than you. It commands a posture: choosing to consider others' interests ahead of your own, as verse 4 clarifies.

## How to apply Philippians 2:3 today

This verse confronts the instinct to make ourselves the center. Practically, it looks like listening before speaking, giving credit freely, serving without needing recognition, and asking what others need rather than only what you want. In a disagreement, it means seeking to understand the other person's concern as genuinely valid. Humility here is not self-hatred but self-forgetfulness — thinking of yourself less so you can think of others more. Jesus modeled it perfectly, and verse 3 invites us to follow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Philippians 2:3 mean I should think I'm worthless?** No. It calls for humility, not self-hatred. "Esteem other better" means counting others' interests and needs as genuinely important — a choice about how you treat people, not a belief that you have no value to God.

**What is the difference between strife and vainglory?** "Strife" (selfish ambition) is acting to win or gain advantage over others; "vainglory" is craving empty praise. Paul rules out both the competitive motive and the show-off motive.

**How is this verse connected to Jesus?** Verses 5–8 immediately hold up Christ as the model: though equal with God, He humbled Himself and became a servant. Paul grounds the call to humility in the example of Jesus Himself.
