**Short answer:** Ezekiel 36:26 is God's promise to replace a hardened, unresponsive "heart of stone" with a soft "heart of flesh" and to put a new spirit within His people. It describes an inner transformation God does — not a self-improvement project, but a heart He remakes so His people can truly know and follow Him.

## The context: hope spoken to exiles

Ezekiel prophesied to Israel during the Babylonian exile, a time of judgment and despair. Yet chapter 36 is one of the great promises of restoration. God says He will gather His scattered people, cleanse them (v. 25), and renew them from the inside out. Verse 26 is the centerpiece: the problem was never merely their circumstances but their hearts, and God promises to change the heart itself.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: **"A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh"** (Ezekiel 36:26).

- **"A new heart... a new spirit"** — In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of the will, mind, and desires. God promises to renew the very core of a person, not just their behavior.
- **"I will put within you"** — This is God's work. The verbs are His: *I will give, I will put, I will take away.* The transformation originates with God.
- **"the stony heart"** — A heart of stone is hard, cold, unresponsive to God — unable to feel or obey.
- **"an heart of flesh"** — Soft, living, responsive. A heart that can be moved by God and can love and follow Him.

The very next verse explains the purpose: "I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes" (Ezekiel 36:27, KJV). The new heart makes genuine obedience possible.

## Cross-references

- **Ezekiel 11:19** — A nearly identical promise of "one heart" and a heart of flesh.
- **Jeremiah 31:33** — God will write His law "in their hearts" (KJV).
- **2 Corinthians 5:17** — In Christ, one is "a new creature: old things are passed away" (KJV).
- **Psalm 51:10** — "Create in me a clean heart, O God" (KJV).

## How to apply Ezekiel 36:26 today

This verse offers hope to anyone who feels they cannot change themselves. Real transformation is something God does within, not something we manufacture by willpower. Many Christians read Ezekiel 36:26 alongside the New Testament's teaching on new birth and pray it back to God — asking Him to soften what has grown hard. If your heart feels cold toward God, this promise says He specializes in giving new ones. The response it invites is not striving but surrender: ask, and cooperate with the work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who does Ezekiel 36:26 apply to?** It was first spoken to exiled Israel as a promise of national and spiritual restoration. Christians also read it as a picture of the inner renewal God gives all who come to Him, echoed in New Testament teaching on the new birth and new creation.

**What is the difference between a heart of stone and a heart of flesh?** A heart of stone is hard and unresponsive to God; a heart of flesh is soft and alive, able to feel, love, and obey. The contrast is between deadness and responsiveness, not literal organs.

**Is the new heart something we produce ourselves?** No. Every verb in the promise belongs to God — He gives, puts in, and removes. It is His work of grace, received rather than achieved, though we respond by walking in it.
