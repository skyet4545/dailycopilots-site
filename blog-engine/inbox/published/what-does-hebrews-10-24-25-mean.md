**Short answer:** Hebrews 10:24–25 means believers should actively encourage one another toward love and good deeds, and should not give up meeting together, but keep gathering to strengthen each other — all the more as the return of Christ draws near. It is a call to committed, mutually encouraging Christian community.

## The context: hold fast together

Hebrews was written to Christians tempted to drift away from their faith under pressure. Just before these verses, the writer says believers can now "draw near" to God with confidence because of Christ's finished work (Hebrews 10:19–22). Then come three encouragements: hold fast our confession (v. 23), stir one another to love (v. 24), and keep meeting together (v. 25). The point is that no one holds fast alone — perseverance happens in community.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: **"And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching"** (Hebrews 10:24–25).

- **"consider one another"** — to pay attention to each other, to think carefully about how to help fellow believers.
- **"provoke unto love and to good works"** — to spur or stir up. We are to be a positive provocation, prompting one another toward love and action.
- **"Not forsaking the assembling"** — do not abandon the habit of gathering. Some were already drifting from the meetings.
- **"exhorting one another"** — encouraging, urging one another to keep going.
- **"as ye see the day approaching"** — the coming Day of the Lord, Christ's return and judgment. The nearer it comes, the more the community is needed.

The verse is often quoted about church attendance, and rightly so — but its heart is deeper than showing up. Gathering is the means; the goal is mutual encouragement that keeps faith alive.

## How to apply Hebrews 10:24-25 today

This passage pushes back on faith practiced in isolation. Applying it means committing to a local body of believers — not merely attending, but showing up ready to encourage. Ask yourself: whom can I spur toward love this week? Who needs a word of exhortation? It also means letting others do the same for you. In seasons when meeting together feels hard, this verse says the harder it gets and the nearer Christ's return, the more we need one another, not less.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Hebrews 10:25 only about church attendance?** Attendance is part of it, but the aim is deeper: mutual encouragement and stirring one another toward love and good works. Gathering is the setting where that encouragement happens, so the two cannot be separated.

**What is "the day" that is approaching?** It refers to the Day of the Lord — Christ's return and final judgment. The writer says that as that day draws nearer, believers need one another's encouragement even more.

**What does it mean to "provoke" one another?** Here "provoke" is positive — to spur or stir up. Believers are called to be a good influence that prompts each other toward love and good deeds, rather than leaving one another to grow cold.
