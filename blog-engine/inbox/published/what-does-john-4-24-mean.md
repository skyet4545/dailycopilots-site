**Short answer:** John 4:24 says that because God is spirit — not confined to a place or a physical form — worship must be genuine inward devotion aligned with what is true about him. Jesus said it to settle a dispute over *where* people should worship by relocating the question to *how*.

"God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth" (KJV).

## The context

Jesus is at Jacob's well near Sychar, in Samaria, speaking with a Samaritan woman (John 4:1–26). The conversation moves from water, to her marital history, to the point where she raises the long-standing religious dispute between their peoples: her ancestors worshipped on this mountain — Mount Gerizim — while Jews insisted worship belonged in Jerusalem.

That was not a diversion. It was the defining split between Jews and Samaritans, centuries old. She was asking the biggest religious question in her world.

Jesus answers in two parts. First, verse 21: an hour is coming when worship will be tied to neither mountain. Second, verse 22: he does not treat the two positions as equivalent — "salvation is of the Jews" (KJV). Then verse 23 announces that the hour has arrived when true worshippers will worship the Father in spirit and in truth, and that the Father seeks such worshippers.

Verse 24 gives the reason. The nature of God determines the nature of acceptable worship.

## Phrase by phrase

**"God is a Spirit."** The Greek has no article — many translations render it "God is spirit," a statement about God's nature rather than a classification. He is not material, not localized, not bounded by geography. This is the premise from which everything else follows: if God is not confined to a mountain, worship cannot be either.

**"They that worship him must worship him."** The word is a strong necessity. This is not preference or style. It is what worship of this God requires by definition.

**"In spirit."** Two readings have long circulated: the human spirit — worship from the inner person rather than external performance — and the Holy Spirit, who enables true worship. John's Gospel supports both; chapter 3 has already tied new birth to the Spirit. Many read verse 24 as involving both, since worship from the heart is itself something the Spirit produces.

**"And in truth."** Worship must correspond to reality — to who God actually is, as he has revealed himself. This is why verse 22 matters. Jesus had just said the Samaritans worshipped what they did not know. Sincerity alone was insufficient; truth about God is part of the requirement.

The two words together form a single standard. Truth without inward reality produces cold correctness. Inward reality without truth produces sincere devotion to something other than God.

## How to apply it today

The verse dismantles the assumption that worship is primarily located. Buildings, styles, and music are not the issue Jesus addresses — he removes the question of place entirely. That cuts against arguments about which service format is more worshipful in either direction.

It equally dismantles the idea that sincerity is sufficient. Verse 22 is uncomfortable and often skipped: Jesus explicitly says one group worshipped in ignorance. "In truth" means worship aims at God as he has actually revealed himself, not at a version assembled to preference.

Practically, this suggests two questions rather than one. Is my worship engaging my actual inner life, or am I present and absent at the same time? And is it directed at the God Scripture describes, including the parts I would not have chosen?

Note also who is being sought. Verse 23 says the Father *seeks* such worshippers. The initiative is God's. This was said to a Samaritan woman with a complicated history, in a place Jews avoided — which is the most striking thing in the passage. The people Jesus went out of his way to find were not the ones with the right address.

**Where faithful Christians differ:** whether "in spirit" refers primarily to the Holy Spirit or the human spirit, and how much the verse regulates corporate worship practice. Some traditions read it as freeing worship from prescribed forms; others hold that Scripture still governs how corporate worship is conducted and that "in truth" implies that regulation. Both agree the verse rules out worship that is merely external.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does "God is a Spirit" mean?**
The Greek lacks an article, so most modern translations render it "God is spirit" — a statement about God's nature. He is immaterial and not confined to any location or physical form. That premise is what makes the rest of the verse follow: an unlocalized God cannot be worshipped in a merely locational way.

**Does "in spirit" mean the Holy Spirit or my spirit?**
Both readings have long support. John's Gospel connects the Spirit to new birth in chapter 3, and Philippians 3:3 speaks of worshipping God in the spirit. Many readers hold both together: worship from the inner person, enabled by the Holy Spirit.

**Why did Jesus say "salvation is of the Jews"?**
In verse 22 he refuses to treat the Samaritan and Jewish positions as equally valid, noting that the Samaritans worshipped what they did not know. It underlines the "in truth" half of verse 24 — sincerity is not by itself the standard. It also affirms that God's redemptive plan came through Israel.

**Does John 4:24 mean church buildings do not matter?**
The verse removes place as the defining question of acceptable worship, which is what the woman had asked about. It does not address whether gathering is valuable — Hebrews 10:24–25 speaks positively about assembling. The point is that no location makes worship acceptable or unacceptable.
