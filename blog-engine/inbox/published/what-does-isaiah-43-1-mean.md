**Short answer:** Isaiah 43:1 means that God tells His people not to fear because He created them, redeemed them, called them personally by name, and claims them as His own. It's a message of belonging and security: the same God who made them has also rescued them and knows them individually.

## The context

Isaiah 40–55 is often called the "Book of Comfort." These chapters speak hope to Israel, looking ahead to a time of exile in Babylon and God's promise to bring His people back. Chapter 42 ends with words about Israel's suffering; chapter 43 opens with "But now" — a sharp turn from judgment to reassurance.

Verse 1 is God directly addressing His people as their Creator and Redeemer.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: "But now thus saith the LORD that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine."

- **"He that created thee... formed thee"** — God is the maker of His people, using language that recalls a potter shaping clay. Their existence is His work.
- **"Fear not"** — the command flows from what follows. There are reasons behind it.
- **"I have redeemed thee"** — redemption pictures buying back or setting free, as a kinsman-redeemer would rescue a family member.
- **"I have called thee by thy name"** — personal, intimate knowledge. God does not relate to His people as a faceless crowd but by name.
- **"Thou art mine"** — belonging. They are God's own possession, and He does not abandon what is His.

The verse addresses "Jacob" and "Israel" — the nation — but its truths about a personal, redeeming, naming God have long comforted individual believers too.

## Cross-references

- Isaiah 41:10 — "Fear not, for I am with you."
- Isaiah 43:2 — the very next verse: through the waters and fire, God is present.
- John 10:3 — the good shepherd "calleth his own sheep by name."
- Exodus 6:6 — God promises to redeem Israel from Egypt.
- 1 Peter 2:9 — believers are "a people for God's own possession."

## How to apply it today

Fear often whispers that we are alone, unnoticed, or unwanted. Isaiah 43:1 answers each of those fears directly: you are made, redeemed, known by name, and claimed.

When anxiety rises, this verse gives a place to stand. It doesn't promise the absence of hardship — the next verse assumes waters and fire — but it grounds courage in identity and belonging. Try personalizing its truths in prayer: God formed me, redeemed me, knows my name, and calls me His. That security is meant to steady the heart before the trouble ever comes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who was Isaiah 43:1 originally written to?**
It was spoken to the people of Israel (called "Jacob" and "Israel"), comforting them with hope of redemption during and after the Babylonian exile. Its portrait of a personal, redeeming God has since encouraged individual believers as well.

**What does "I have called you by name" mean?**
It expresses intimate, personal knowledge — God relates to His people individually, not as an anonymous mass. Jesus echoes this when He says the good shepherd calls his own sheep by name (John 10:3).

**Does "you are mine" mean God owns His people?**
It expresses belonging and covenant relationship, not ownership in a harsh sense. It assures God's people that they are His treasured possession, so He will not abandon them — a theme picked up in 1 Peter 2:9.
