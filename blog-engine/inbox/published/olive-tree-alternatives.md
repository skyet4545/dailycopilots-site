If you are looking to replace Olive Tree, the right answer depends on which part of it you are actually paying for. **Blue Letter Bible** is the best free alternative for most people — it gives away the Strong's, lexicon, interlinear, and commentary layer that Olive Tree sells as add-ons. **YouVersion** is the better pick if you mostly read and follow plans. **Logos** is the upgrade if you preach or study academically and want a library that goes deep. **Accordance** is the one to look at if what bothers you about Olive Tree is renting instead of owning. **e-Sword** is the strongest free desktop-style study environment. And if the real problem is that a reference app hands you resources but never explains the passage, an AI study tool like **Bible Copilot** targets a gap none of these fill.

## Why people look for an Olive Tree alternative

Olive Tree is a genuinely good app. Its reader is clean, its offline support is excellent — Bible, audio, and study tools all work with no connection — and it runs on iPhone, iPad, Android, Mac, and Windows with syncing between them. Most people who shop around are not unhappy with the reader. They are reacting to cost.

Two things push them out. First, the resource model: the app is free, but study Bibles, commentaries, dictionaries, and language tools are bought individually, and a serious library adds up. Second, subscription creep: Olive Tree's subscription runs $5.99/month, $29.99 for six months, or $59.99/year after a 14-day trial, with specialized bundles layered on top — Commentary Select at roughly $8.99–$19.99/month, a Classic Commentary tier around $7.99/month.

To Olive Tree's credit, its à-la-carte purchases are genuine purchases: what you buy, you keep. The question is whether you need to pay for what you are paying for — because several of these tools are free.

## Blue Letter Bible — the closest free replacement

For most people leaving Olive Tree over cost, this is the answer. Blue Letter Bible offers 30+ translations and lets you tap any word to see the underlying Greek or Hebrew, with Strong's numbers linked to full lexicon entries — Thayer's for Greek, Brown-Driver-Briggs for Hebrew — plus thousands of text commentaries. No premium tier, no in-app purchase, no ads, no account.

Put plainly: the study layer Olive Tree sells piece by piece, Blue Letter Bible gives away. The trade-off is design — it was built by people who care about tools more than interfaces, and next to Olive Tree's polished reader it feels dense.

## YouVersion — if you mostly read

If Olive Tree is where you read a chapter a day and follow a plan, YouVersion does that better and costs nothing. Thousands of versions across thousands of languages, audio, reading plans, offline downloads, and a community layer for reading with friends — all free and ad-free.

What it does not have is a study library — no lexicons, no serious commentary set. It is the best reading app in the category and does not pretend otherwise, which is why most people pair it with a second tool.

## Bible Hub and STEP Bible — free original-language depth

Both are free and both go deeper on the languages than Olive Tree's free tier. **Bible Hub** is a web study suite with interlinears, concordances, lexicons, and a large stack of parallel commentaries; its strength is sheer quantity, its weakness is that it was built for the desktop web. **STEP Bible**, from researchers at Tyndale House in Cambridge, has arguably the best free collection of biblical texts in the ancient languages, runs offline in a browser, and carries no ads or in-app purchases.

## Logos — if you preach or study academically

Logos is the step up, not the step sideways. Its 2026 tiers are Premium at $9.99/month, Pro at $14.99/month, and Max at $19.99/month, or $99.99, $149.99, and $199.99 annually. Pro and Max add Mobile Ed courses and a sermons collection; all tiers include a storewide discount and a free book each month.

You are paying for library depth and research tooling Olive Tree does not attempt to match. If you prepare sermons weekly or write at a seminary level, the cost is defensible. For a lay reader it is overkill — and more expensive than what you left.

## Accordance — if you want to own, not rent

Accordance runs on the opposite philosophy from subscriptions: what you buy is yours permanently. Packages start around $49.90 for a Basic Starter collection — ESV, KJV with Strong's, a Greek New Testament, a Hebrew Bible, Nave's Topical Bible — and scale up steeply for research-grade libraries. The mobile app is free with in-app purchases, and there is a 90-day trial on the starter collection.

Its original-language search engine is the real advantage, and it is a favorite among people who work in Greek and Hebrew regularly. It is not the friendliest first study app.

## e-Sword — the free desktop workhorse

e-Sword has been the free Bible study standard on Windows for years and is still actively developed — version 15.1.0 shipped in July 2026 with a major redesign. The free module library is enormous: translations, commentaries, dictionaries, and lexicons, all downloadable and fully offline. Mobile versions exist (published by Tecarta), though desktop is where it is strongest. If your Olive Tree use is study at a desk rather than reading on a phone, this replaces a lot of paid resources with free ones.

## Bible Copilot — when you want the passage explained

Every app above is a *reference*. They hand you the text, the lexicon entry, the commentary, and leave the interpreting to you. That is correct design for a reference — and it is not what most people want at 11pm staring at a confusing verse in Romans.

Bible Copilot targets that moment. You ask about a passage and get a structured answer through six modes — Summary, Observe, Interpret, Theology, Apply, and Apologetics — following the inductive order of observation before interpretation before application. Every answer cites the Scripture it draws from, so you can check it, in the spirit of the Bereans who "searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so" (Acts 17:11, KJV). It is free for 3 questions a day with no account; Pro is $9.99/month or $49.99/year with a 7-day trial. You can [get it on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6758913373).

Two honest limits: it is iPhone and iPad only, and it is not a resource library — there is no shelf of purchased commentaries behind it. It complements a reader rather than replacing one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I lose my Olive Tree books if I switch apps?**
No. Purchases stay in your Olive Tree account and remain readable in that app, which is free to keep installed. Olive Tree's documentation also says you retain access to subscription resources you previously used after a subscription ends. What you cannot do is export them into a different app — no publisher in this category allows that. Keep Olive Tree installed as an archive.

**What is the best free Olive Tree alternative?**
Blue Letter Bible for study, YouVersion for reading. Between them you cover most of what Olive Tree charges for. If you work at a desktop, add e-Sword. All three are free with no ads or required purchases.

**Is Logos worth the jump from Olive Tree?**
Only if you use it weekly for teaching, preaching, or academic work. At $9.99–$19.99/month it costs more than Olive Tree's subscription, and the value is entirely in library depth. A reader who just wants a study Bible and a commentary gets there far cheaper.

**Which alternative is best for Greek and Hebrew?**
Accordance if you have training and want to own a serious library; Blue Letter Bible, Bible Hub, or STEP Bible for the same lookups free. STEP Bible in particular designs its interlinear for readers with no language training.

**Can an AI Bible app replace a study Bible?**
Not in the sense of replacing curated scholarship — a study Bible's notes were written and reviewed by named scholars, and that accountability matters. An AI tool is better understood as a study partner that points you back to the text. Verify what it says against Scripture and, where doctrine is at stake, against your own church tradition.

## How to choose

Pick by the job, not the brand. Reading and plans: YouVersion. Free study tools: Blue Letter Bible. Free desktop depth: e-Sword. Owning a library: Accordance. Preaching and academic work: Logos. A hard passage explained on your phone: an AI study app. Most people end up with two apps, and that pair usually costs less than the subscription they were replacing.
