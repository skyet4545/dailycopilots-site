Family is one of the most consistent themes in Scripture, from the first household in Genesis to the "household of God" described in the New Testament. Here are several verses that speak directly to family life.

On children as a gift: "Behold, children are a heritage from the LORD, the fruit of the womb a reward" (Psalm 127:3, ESV). Before family is ever described as a duty, it's described as a gift - something received, not just managed.

On raising children well: "And these words that I command you today shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise" (Deuteronomy 6:6-7, ESV). Spiritual formation in a family isn't confined to a single lesson or church service - it happens in ordinary conversation, woven through daily life.

On family unity: "Behold, how good and pleasant it is when brothers dwell in unity!" (Psalm 133:1, ESV). Written about the joy of God's covenant people living in harmony, it applies just as directly to households learning to forgive and stay close despite friction.

On honoring parents: "Honor your father and your mother, that your days may be long in the land that the LORD your God is giving you" (Exodus 20:12, ESV). This is one of the Ten Commandments, showing that respect within family relationships is a core part of a life ordered rightly before God.

On a household's example of faith: "But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" (Joshua 24:15, ESV). Joshua's declaration reminds every household leader that faith is meant to be modeled, not just professed.

On patient love within a family: "Love is patient and kind... it does not insist on its own way... Love bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things" (1 Corinthians 13:4-7, ESV). Few relationships test patience and forgiveness more than family - and few describe what love should look like more clearly than this passage.

Family relationships are rarely simple, but Scripture consistently frames them as a place for God's love to be practiced in the most ordinary, daily ways.
