## The Verse

"For we walk by faith, not by sight." (2 Corinthians 5:7, ESV)

## The Context

Paul writes this in the middle of a passage about the hope of resurrection and eternal life. Just before it, he says, "So we are always of good courage. We know that while we are at home in the body we are away from the LORD" (2 Corinthians 5:6). Paul is contrasting present, visible circumstances — the "earthly tent" of the body he describes earlier in the chapter (5:1) — with the unseen reality of what God has promised. "Walking by faith" isn't a throwaway motivational phrase here; it's Paul's explanation for why he and his fellow believers can stay confident even while suffering hardship that has no visible resolution yet.

## What "Sight" and "Faith" Mean Here

"Sight" refers to what can currently be observed and verified — the tangible, present situation. "Faith" is trust grounded in what God has said, even when it hasn't yet become visible. Paul isn't saying evidence or reason don't matter; he's saying that a Christian's confidence is anchored in God's revealed promises rather than exclusively in whatever the current circumstances seem to indicate. The Greek word for "sight" here (eidos) suggests outward appearance or form — what presents itself to the eye — while "faith" (pistis) is settled trust.

## How This Applies

This verse gets quoted often in seasons of uncertainty — illness, financial strain, an unanswered prayer — because it doesn't promise that circumstances will immediately look better. It promises that God's character and promises remain trustworthy even when the visible facts are discouraging. Paul himself wrote this letter having endured beatings, shipwreck, and hardship (2 Corinthians 11:23-27), so this isn't detached theory; it's a conclusion drawn from lived experience. The verse invites a shift in what a person treats as final authority: not the momentary read of a situation, but the steady promises of God.
