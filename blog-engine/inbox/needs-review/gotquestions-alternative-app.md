The honest short answer: if you like GotQuestions and just want verse-by-verse coverage instead of topical Q&A, **BibleRef** is the closest alternative — free, from the same ministry, walking through Scripture verse by verse. To dig into the original text yourself, **Blue Letter Bible** is the best free option, with Strong's numbers, lexicons, and thousands of classic commentaries. To get *the specific verse you are stuck on* explained right now, an AI study tool like **Bible Copilot** is the fastest route. **Logos** wins on depth if you will pay for a library. **Bible Chat** is the conversational option — with the messiest pricing here.

Worth saying up front: most people searching for a "GotQuestions alternative" do not dislike GotQuestions. They hit its real limit — it answers a fixed library of questions someone else wrote, and theirs was not in it.

## What GotQuestions actually is (and where it stops)

The GotQuestions app is free, from Got Questions Ministries, on iOS, Android, and Amazon. It holds more than 10,000 pre-written Bible and theology articles, works fully offline once downloaded, supports search and bookmarks, and lets you submit a question to their volunteer team. On the US App Store it sits at roughly 4.4 stars across about 744 ratings.

It is genuinely excellent for topical questions — "what does the Bible say about divorce," "is the rapture biblical," "who was Melchizedek" — answered from a conservative, evangelical, Protestant perspective the ministry states openly.

The limits are structural, not quality problems:

- It is organized by **question**, not by **verse**. There is no article for most individual verses.
- Answers are pre-written. If your wording does not match an existing article, search comes up short.
- Submitting a new question means waiting for a human reply, not an answer in the moment.
- It is a reference library, not a study workflow. It will not walk you through a passage.

## BibleRef — the closest thing to "GotQuestions, but per verse"

BibleRef is also produced by Got Questions Ministries, which makes it the least jarring switch. It is free, on iOS, Android, and the web, giving verse-by-verse and chapter-level commentary with cross-references. Each verse page shows the text in several major translations side by side (ESV, NIV, NASB, CSB, NLT, KJV, NKJV). Its stated approach is to avoid jargon and denominational agendas, and to present multiple legitimate readings honestly when a passage is genuinely debated.

The catch: coverage is still being built out book by book, so it is not yet complete across all 66 books and some passages have no entry. Check your book before committing to it as your main tool.

## Blue Letter Bible — the best free option for doing the work yourself

Blue Letter Bible carries 30-plus translations, Hebrew and Greek interlinears tied to Strong's Concordance, thousands of text commentaries from dozens of authors, dictionaries, the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge, and audio teaching — most of it usable offline, none of it paywalled. Tap a word in a verse and you get its Strong's entry, parsing data, lexicon definition, and classical commentary on the passage. In 2026 the platform added AI-assisted verse explanations and sermons mapped to specific passages.

The tradeoff: it hands you sources, not conclusions. If you want to learn to interpret, that is a feature. If you want a direct answer to "what does this mean," it is more work than GotQuestions was.

## Bible Copilot — for the question GotQuestions did not have an article for

This is where an AI study app earns its place: instead of hunting for a pre-written article that matches your question, you ask about the verse in front of you.

It runs six study modes on any passage — Summary, Observe, Interpret, Theology, Apply, and Apologetics — following the inductive method taught in seminary: see what the text says before deciding what it means, and settle what it means before deciding what to do about it. Answers cite Scripture rather than floating free of it. It is free for three questions a day with no account required, and Pro is $9.99/month or $49.99/year with a 7-day trial. On iPhone you can [get Bible Copilot on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6758913373).

Two honest caveats. It is **iOS only** — Android users should take BibleRef or Blue Letter Bible instead. And it is an AI tool: a strong study partner, a poor final authority. Verify anything doctrinally weighty against the text and a trusted teacher — true of every AI Bible app, including those that do not say so.

## Logos — depth, if you will pay for it

Logos is the heavyweight: an enormous commentary and original-language library with an AI Study Assistant that answers by citing books you actually own. Subscription tiers in 2026 run Premium $9.99/month, Pro $14.99/month, and Max $19.99/month, with annual pricing lowering the effective rate and a free trial available.

If you preach, teach, or study academically, nothing here matches it for depth. If you just wanted to know what Romans 8:28 means on a Tuesday night, it is far more app than you need.

## Bible Chat — conversational, but read the price tag

Bible Chat is the most conversational option: a chat-style AI on iOS and Android that answers questions and offers devotional guidance in a warm, pastoral tone. Many people find that easier to talk to than a reference library.

Be careful with pricing. Reported plans include roughly $4.99/week, $12.99/month, and $59.99/year, and the price you see varies by which paywall variant the app serves you. A weekly plan can quietly cost several times an annual one — look for the annual option first.

## The most practical answer

It is not either/or. Keep GotQuestions — it is free, works offline, and is still one of the best topical Bible references available. Then add one tool for the verses it does not cover: BibleRef for the same voice, Blue Letter Bible for the evidence, Bible Copilot for your own question answered on the spot.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the GotQuestions app free?**

Yes — free on iOS, Android, and the Amazon App Store, with no in-app purchases listed. Its full article database works offline once downloaded.

**What is the best free GotQuestions alternative?**

For verse-by-verse commentary, BibleRef — free, from the same ministry, on iOS, Android, and web. For original-language study and classic commentaries, Blue Letter Bible, also free with no paywalled core features.

**Is there a GotQuestions app that answers questions with AI?**

Not GotQuestions itself — its articles are written by people, and new questions go to a volunteer team. For instant AI answers grounded in Scripture, Bible Copilot, Logos' AI Study Assistant, and Bible Chat all do this, with different tradeoffs in depth, price, and platform.

**Can an AI Bible app be trusted doctrinally?**

Treat it as a study partner, not an authority. Insist that it cites the actual biblical text and flags genuinely disputed passages instead of picking a side silently, and check anything significant against Scripture and a trusted teacher in your church. AI tools can be confidently wrong, which is harder to catch than obvious wrongness.

**What is the difference between GotQuestions and BibleRef?**

Both come from Got Questions Ministries. GotQuestions is organized by topical question; BibleRef is organized by passage, with verse-by-verse and chapter commentary. Use GotQuestions for "what does the Bible say about X" and BibleRef for "what does this verse mean."

**Do I need to pay to get a verse explained?**

No. BibleRef and Blue Letter Bible are free with no subscription, and Bible Copilot's free tier allows three questions a day without an account. Paid tools mainly buy volume and library depth, not the basics.
