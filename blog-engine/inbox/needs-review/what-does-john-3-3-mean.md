"Jesus answered him, 'Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God'" (John 3:3, ESV).

Jesus speaks these words to Nicodemus, a Pharisee and "a ruler of the Jews" (3:1) who comes to Jesus at night, acknowledging that Jesus is "a teacher come from God" because of the signs he has performed. Jesus doesn't respond to Nicodemus's compliment directly — he goes straight to the core issue, telling this respected religious leader that his existing religious credentials, however impressive, are not the entry point into God's kingdom at all. Something more fundamental is required: a new beginning as total as physical birth itself.

Nicodemus takes the phrase literally and gets stuck on the impossibility: "How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb and be born?" (3:4). Jesus clarifies that he means a spiritual rebirth, not a biological one: "unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God. That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit" (3:5-6). The Greek phrase translated "born again" (gennethe anothen) can also mean "born from above," and both senses are likely intended — a birth that is both a second beginning and one that originates with God rather than human effort.

Jesus then compares the Spirit's work to wind: "The wind blows where it wishes, and you hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it goes. So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit" (3:8) — invisible in its mechanics, but unmistakable in its effect. The new birth isn't something a person achieves through religious performance, ethnic heritage, or moral effort; it's something God's Spirit does, comparable to an unseen force producing a visible result.

The passage's most famous verse follows a few lines later: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life" (3:16) — tying the new birth directly to belief in Jesus rather than to any ritual or lineage. Read together, John 3:3-16 teaches that entering God's kingdom requires a Spirit-given transformation available to anyone who believes, not a status inherited or earned — which is precisely why Jesus tells even a respected, sincere religious teacher like Nicodemus that he must start over.
