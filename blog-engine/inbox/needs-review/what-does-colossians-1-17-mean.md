**Short answer:** Colossians 1:17 makes two claims about Christ: he existed before all created things ("he is before all things"), and he actively holds the universe together right now ("in him all things hold together"). It is the center point of a short hymn-like passage (1:15-20) that presents Christ as both the agent of creation and the force sustaining it moment to moment.

## The context: the middle of a hymn to Christ's supremacy

Colossians 1:15-20 is widely recognized as one of the densest theological passages in Paul's letters, likely drawing on early Christian hymn material. Paul wrote to a church in Colossae that was being pulled toward teachings that diminished Christ's status — treating him as one spiritual power among several rather than supreme over all of them. The passage answers that directly: verse 15 calls Christ "the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation"; verse 16 says "by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible"; verse 17 sits between the creation claim and the reconciliation claim that follows in verses 18-20, forming the hinge of the whole hymn.

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The ESV reads: "And he is before all things, and in him all things hold together."

- **"He is before all things"** — this states priority both in time and in rank. Christ exists prior to everything that was made (implying he himself is not part of the created order, consistent with verse 16's claim that all things were created "by him"), and he holds a position of supremacy over all things.
- **"In him all things hold together"** — the Greek verb here (synesteken) carries the sense of being held in a coherent, unified state, not merely "existing." Paul is not describing a one-time act of creation followed by a universe left to run on its own; he is describing continuous, present-tense sustaining. The universe's ongoing coherence — the fact that it holds together rather than flying apart — is attributed directly to Christ.

## Cross-references

- **John 1:3** — "All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made." The same claim of Christ's role in creation, in different wording.
- **Hebrews 1:3** — Christ is described as "upholding the universe by the word of his power," a very close parallel to "holding together" in Colossians 1:17, emphasizing ongoing sustaining rather than a completed past action.
- **1 Corinthians 8:6** — "one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and through whom we exist," another early statement of Christ's role in the existence of everything.
- **Colossians 1:16** — the verse immediately before, listing what was created "by him and for him," setting up the priority claim of verse 17.

## How to apply it today

Colossians 1:17 is often quoted as evidence for Christ's pre-existence and deity, and that reading is accurate, but the "hold together" clause has a further, practical implication worth noticing: the passage does not describe a distant, hands-off Creator who set the universe in motion and stepped back. It describes ongoing, active sustaining — the coherence of the physical universe at every moment is tied, in Paul's argument, to Christ's continuing role. Read alongside the church-specific application in verse 18 ("he is the head of the body, the church"), the logic of the hymn moves from cosmic scope (all creation) to a more intimate scope (the church) without changing the underlying claim: the same Christ who holds the universe together is the one holding his church together.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does "before all things" just mean Christ is older than everything?**
It carries that sense, but also rank and priority — "before" in the sense of supreme over, not merely temporally earlier. Both senses are usually read together given the hymn's overall argument for Christ's supremacy.

**What does "hold together" actually mean scientifically or physically?**
Paul isn't making a scientific claim about physics; he's making a theological claim that the coherence and continuation of creation depends on Christ's sustaining role, a claim paralleled in Hebrews 1:3's "upholding the universe by the word of his power."

**How does Colossians 1:17 relate to the rest of the hymn in 1:15-20?**
Verse 17 is the hinge: verses 15-16 establish Christ's role in creation, verse 17 states his priority and sustaining role, and verses 18-20 turn to his headship over the church and his role in reconciliation through the cross.
