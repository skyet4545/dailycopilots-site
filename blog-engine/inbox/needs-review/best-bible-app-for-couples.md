For most married couples, the right answer is **YouVersion** — completely free, and its "Plans with Friends" feature is the best tool for reading Scripture together: you both see each other's progress and get a private discussion thread on each day's reading. Start there before paying for anything.

If you want something written specifically *for marriage* rather than a general Bible app pointed at it, **Covenant** is the best purpose-built Christian option and the cheapest paid pick at $23.90/year. If your evenings are the only time you connect, **Abide** is strongest for praying together. And if the real problem is recurring conflict rather than devotional consistency, **Lasting** will help more than any devotional — with the honest caveat that it is secular and research-based, not Scripture-based.

One thing worth naming up front: most "couples Bible apps" are devotional apps — a short reading and a reflection. Very few will explain a passage when the two of you disagree about what it means, which is where a lot of couples actually stall out.

## YouVersion — start here, and possibly stop here

There is no good reason to pay before trying this one. YouVersion is free with no ads and no paid tier, and its plan catalog includes a deep bench of marriage, engagement, and communication plans, usually five to thirty days long.

What makes it a genuine *couples* app rather than a solo one is Plans with Friends. Either of you starts a plan and invites the other; you both see when the other completes a day, and each day gets a private discussion area that works like a group text. That solves the real failure mode of couples devotionals — one spouse quietly drifts three days behind and nobody says anything.

Its limits are real: it hands you the passage and a devotional thought and stops. It will not explain a difficult text.

## Covenant — the best purpose-built option

Covenant is built for Christian marriage from the ground up: Scripture-rooted daily couple devotionals, prayers, conversation questions, a shared love diary, a special-days calendar, and a widget. It fits if you want the app to supply the structure — something to read and something to talk about, ready every day. It is also the cheapest paid pick by a wide margin: $14.99 for six months, $23.90 per year, or $47.99 for lifetime access.

Be aware it is young, with around 44 App Store ratings averaging 4.3 stars at the time of writing. Try the short plan before buying lifetime.

## Abide — for couples who pray better than they read

Abide is guided Scripture meditation: you pick a theme and a narrator walks you through a two-to-thirty-minute session pairing verses with slow breathing prompts. Marriage is one of its themes, alongside anxiety, parenting, grief, and sleep.

It fits one specific kind of couple — the one where sitting down to read together never quite happens, but lying in bed with something playing does. Pricing is a 7-day trial, then $9.99/month or $39.99/year. It is audio-first, so if you want to read or study, look elsewhere.

## Lasting — the honest recommendation that is not a Bible app

If your pressure point is a fight you keep having rather than a devotional habit you keep dropping, a devotional app is the wrong tool. Lasting has you each work through guided exercises separately, then compare answers — a format that surfaces the gap between what you each assumed the other thought.

It is secular, with no Scripture in it: a 7-day trial, then tiers varying by plan length, commonly around $11.99/month or roughly $79.99 annually. It rates 4.7 across 25,000+ ratings, the strongest track record here. Including it is deliberate — a good counseling tool plus a free YouVersion plan beats a mediocre app that is only nominally Christian.

## Ultimate Intimacy — a real category, handled candidly

Ultimate Intimacy covers the part of marriage most Christian apps route around: physical intimacy and the conversations that go with it. It offers 300+ conversation starters spanning marriage, finances, family, and spiritual topics, an encrypted couples chat, a love-language quiz, and an intimacy game with adjustable levels. It is rated 18+ and explicitly for married couples.

Pricing is $14.99/month, $49.99/year, or $49.99 as a one-time unlock — take the one-time option. It carries 4.9 stars across 2,400+ ratings. It is not a Bible study app and does not claim to be; some couples will find the framing right and others uncomfortable.

## Bible Copilot — when the passage itself is the argument

Marriage passages are among the most contested in Scripture. Ephesians 5, 1 Corinthians 7, 1 Peter 3, Malachi 2 — couples rarely disagree about *whether* to read them, but about what they require. A devotional app hands you a reflection and moves on.

Bible Copilot runs any passage through six study modes — Summary, Observe, Interpret, Theology, Apply, and Apologetics — following the inductive method of observing what the text says before deciding what it means. Every answer cites Scripture, so you can check it rather than take it on trust. Running Ephesians 5:22–33 through Observe and then Interpret together tends to be a more productive twenty minutes than trading opinions.

It is free for three questions a day with no account required, enough to test whether it helps; Pro is $9.99/month or $49.99/year with a 7-day trial, and you can [get it on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6758913373). Two real limits: iPhone and iPad only, and no shared two-person mode — you would use it on one phone, together.

## Two more worth knowing about

**The Marriage App** is a free iPhone devotional from Drs. Paul and Virginia Friesen, who hold doctorates in marriage and family therapy. It rates 4.7 stars, but its last update was in 2017 — a well-written archive, not a supported app. **Love & Scripture** tops several "best couples Bible app" lists, but is still in beta with an email waitlist rather than a shipping app; any list presenting it as available has not checked.

## Which one should you actually pick?

- **You have never done this together:** YouVersion, a 7-day marriage plan, free.
- **You want it decided for you every day:** Covenant.
- **You connect at night, not at the table:** Abide.
- **You keep having the same fight:** Lasting, plus a free YouVersion plan.
- **The intimacy conversation is the stuck one:** Ultimate Intimacy.
- **You disagree about what a passage means:** Bible Copilot.

Scripture frames marriage as a joining: "Therefore a man will leave his father and his mother, and will join with his wife, and they will be one flesh" (Genesis 2:24, WEB). No app does that work. The good ones just make it likelier you show up for it on a Tuesday.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best free Bible app for couples?**
YouVersion, without close competition. It is entirely free with no ads and no premium tier, carries a large catalog of marriage reading plans, and Plans with Friends lets you share a plan, see each other's progress, and discuss each day privately. The Marriage App is also free but has not been updated since 2017.

**Do we need an app built specifically for couples?**
Usually not — a shared YouVersion plan covers what most couples need. A purpose-built app like Covenant is worth it if you want marriage-specific prompts supplied daily without either of you having to choose them. That convenience is what $23.90 a year buys.

**Can a Bible app help if we're actually struggling, not just inconsistent?**
It can help, but it is not the main tool. Persistent conflict responds better to structured counseling exercises — Lasting is the best-reviewed app of that kind — and to a pastor or licensed counselor. Devotional apps build a habit; they do not resolve a pattern.

**Is there a good couples Bible app for Android?**
YouVersion and Abide are both on Android. Covenant, Ultimate Intimacy, Lasting, and Bible Copilot are iOS-only or iOS-first, so mixed-device couples should build around YouVersion as the shared layer.

**What do we do when we disagree about what a marriage passage means?**
Separate the questions: what the text says, then what it meant in its own context, then what it asks of you. Skipping to the third step is where most arguments start. A tool that observes before interpreting and cites its Scripture, like Bible Copilot, helps; so does reading commentaries from different traditions. Faithful Christians have long read Ephesians 5 differently, and an app should show you the reasoning rather than settle it for you.
