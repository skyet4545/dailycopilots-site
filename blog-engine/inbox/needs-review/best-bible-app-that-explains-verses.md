- **Best AI verse explanation on iPhone: Bible Copilot.** Six structured study modes (Summary, Observe, Interpret, Theology, Apply, Apologetics) instead of an open-ended chatbot, with the Scripture it reasons from cited so you can check it. Free for three questions a day, no account.

## What "explains a verse" actually means

Three different products get sold under that phrase, and picking the wrong one is why people bounce off Bible apps.

**A commentary** explains the questions its author chose to answer, usually at the paragraph or chapter level. It's deep, it's accountable, and it may never touch the clause you're stuck on.

**An AI chat** answers *your* question in seconds. The risk is equally real: language models fabricate references. YouVersion's leadership has been publicly blunt that even strong models misquote Scripture a large share of the time — one reason they've kept human curation at the center of their app.

**A study method** is the third option, and the least common. Instead of a blank chat box, the app walks a passage through fixed steps: what does it say, what did it mean, what does it teach, how does it apply.

## Bible Copilot — structured AI, cited

Bible Copilot sits in that third category. Its six modes — Summary, Observe, Interpret, Theology, Apply, and Apologetics — follow the inductive method rather than free-form chat, and each answer cites the Scripture it's built on. Practically, that means you can verify it: read the verses it leaned on and decide whether the reading holds.

The free tier is three questions a day with no account and no email wall, which is enough to test the explanations before you pay. Pro is $9.99/month or $49.99/year with a 7-day trial, and you can [get it on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6758913373) if you're on iPhone.

Honest limits: iOS only, no Android or web. It ships no commentary library, so it will never hand you Guzik or Keller. And it's still an AI — which is precisely why the citations matter more than the prose.

## Blue Letter Bible — the free answer most people should try first

If your budget is zero and you don't want AI in the loop, start here. Blue Letter Bible gives away everything: Strong's numbers, interlinear Greek and Hebrew, lexicons, cross-references, and a very large commentary stack — with no premium tier behind it. In 2026 it added Scofield's study notes and a SermonAudio integration.

The trade-off nobody mentions: much of the free commentary is 18th- and 19th-century. Matthew Henry and Jamieson-Fausset-Brown are excellent and also predate a century of manuscript and archaeological work. David Guzik's modern set helps balance that.

## Logos — the best AI answer, at a real price

Logos' Study Assistant is the strongest version of AI verse explanation available, for one structural reason: it answers from a curated library of real scholarship rather than from open-web training data, and it shows its sources. It requires the Premium tier or higher — $9.99/month, or $99.99/year — and there's a genuine learning curve. If you open it twice a year, you won't get your money back.

## Bible Chat and Faith Guide — the consumer end

Bible Chat is the category's commercial leader and it earns some of that: strong onboarding, widgets, audio Bible, and a broad devotional library. Two cautions. Its weekly billing option is the most aggressive pricing in the category and compounds well past the monthly rate. And independent reviewers through 2026 have repeatedly flagged it for quoting a verse correctly while attaching the wrong reference — an error most readers won't catch.

Faith Guide markets itself as unlimited and free forever. That's the company's own claim, not a verified audit; read the current terms before making it your primary study tool.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best free Bible app that explains verses?**
Blue Letter Bible, if you want human-written commentary — it's genuinely free with no premium tier. If you specifically want AI explanation for free, Bible Copilot gives three questions a day with no account, and Faith Guide advertises unlimited free chat.

**Can AI be trusted to explain the Bible?**
Not blindly. Language models can quote a verse accurately and still cite the wrong chapter, or state a contested interpretation as settled fact. Use apps that cite Scripture and sources so you can verify, treat every answer as a starting point, and check it against the text and, where it matters, your pastor or a trusted commentary.

**Does YouVersion explain verses?**
Not really. YouVersion is outstanding for reading, plans, and community, and it offers guided reading and tappable cross-references — but it has deliberately not shipped an AI verse-explanation feature, citing accuracy concerns. Pair it with a study app rather than expecting one app to do both.

**Is Logos worth it just for verse explanations?**
Only if you teach or preach regularly. At $9.99–$19.99/month it's priced for weekly preparation. For occasional questions, a free commentary site or a cheaper AI study app covers the same ground.

**What about denominational bias in AI answers?**
Every explanation carries assumptions — human commentaries included. On genuinely disputed passages (baptism, church government, the end times), a good tool should say the church has read this more than one way rather than pick a side quietly. If an app never acknowledges disagreement, that's a signal to check its answers against your own tradition.
