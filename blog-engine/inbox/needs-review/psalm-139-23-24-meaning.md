## What Psalm 139:23-24 Says

> "Search me, O God, and know my heart! Try me and know my thoughts! And see if there be any grievous way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting!" (Psalm 139:23-24, ESV)

These two verses close out one of the most personal psalms in the Bible — a psalm that spends its first twenty-two verses marveling that God already knows everything about David: his thoughts before he speaks them (v. 2), his path and his lying down (v. 3), every day of his life written before one of them came to be (v. 16). By the time David reaches verse 23, he isn't introducing new information to God. He's asking God to use what God already knows.

## Why David Prays This

Psalm 139 was likely written while David faced false accusations — "herein he appeals to the heart-searching God for his innocence," as older commentators put it. That context matters, because it means this isn't a prayer of guilt or self-flagellation. It's a prayer of confidence: David is so sure of God's complete knowledge of him that he invites the deepest possible scrutiny, trusting that what's genuinely wrong in him will be shown to him, not held against him in secret.

The Hebrew word behind "search" (chaqar) means to dig down, to probe to the very core of something — the same intensity used elsewhere for searching out a hidden treasure or investigating a difficult case. David isn't asking for a surface-level check-in. He's asking God to go all the way down, past the parts of himself he's aware of, to "any grievous way" — anything that leads away from life, even if David himself hasn't noticed it yet.

## How to Pray It Today

This is a prayer you can genuinely mean without pretending to already know what's wrong. Something like: "God, I don't fully know my own heart. Show me what you see. Where I've drifted, where I've justified something I shouldn't have, where fear or pride is steering me — I'd rather know now than keep walking that way. Lead me in the way everlasting."

That last phrase is the actual point of the prayer. David isn't asking to be examined for its own sake; he's asking to be examined so he can be redirected. The searching isn't the destination — "the way everlasting" is.
