**Short answer:** Acts 16:31 records Paul and Silas's answer to the Philippian jailer's question "What must I do to be saved?" — "Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household." It is a direct statement of the terms of salvation (faith in Jesus) given in the middle of one of the most dramatic conversion narratives in Acts, and "you and your household" points forward to what happens next in the story rather than promising automatic salvation for family members based on one person's faith.

## The context: an earthquake, a near-suicide, and a midnight question

Acts 16 tells the story of Paul and Silas being beaten and imprisoned in Philippi after casting a spirit out of a slave girl. At midnight, while praying and singing hymns, an earthquake shakes the prison doors open and loosens everyone's chains (16:25-26). The jailer, waking to find the doors open, assumes the prisoners have escaped and draws his sword to kill himself — Roman law held guards responsible with their own lives for escaped prisoners. Paul stops him by calling out that all the prisoners are still there (16:28). Trembling, the jailer falls before Paul and Silas and asks the question that verse 31 answers: "Sirs, what must I do to be saved?" (16:30).

## What it means, phrase by phrase

The ESV reads: "And they said, 'Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household.'"

- **"Believe in the Lord Jesus"** — Paul and Silas give a direct, uncomplicated answer to a direct question. The condition for salvation is stated as faith in Jesus, consistent with Paul's teaching elsewhere (Romans 10:9, Ephesians 2:8-9) that salvation comes through belief rather than through ritual or earned merit.
- **"You will be saved"** — the promise is addressed to the jailer personally in response to his personal question.
- **"You and your household"** — this phrase should be read alongside what happens immediately afterward: verse 32 says Paul and Silas "spoke the word of the Lord to him and to all who were in his house," and verse 34 says the jailer "rejoiced along with his entire household that he had believed in God." The household's salvation is tied to the household also hearing the word and believing, not granted automatically because the head of the household believed. The phrase is best read as identifying who the offer extends to (the jailer's whole family, servants included, is welcome to respond) rather than promising salvation to unbelieving family members based on someone else's faith.

## Cross-references

- **Acts 11:14** — Peter's earlier account of Cornelius, told a message "by which you will be saved, you and all your household," another case where the household is present and hears the message directly.
- **Romans 10:9** — "if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved," the same core condition of belief stated in Paul's later letter.
- **Acts 16:32-34** — the verses immediately following, showing the household hearing the word, believing, and being baptized together that same night — the narrative's own explanation of how "you and your household" plays out.

## How to apply it today

Acts 16:31 is sometimes read as a promise that a believing parent guarantees the salvation of the rest of the family regardless of the family's own response. The passage's own next few verses argue against that reading: the household is not saved by proxy, but because Paul and Silas immediately speak the word to everyone in the house, and everyone in the house believes and is baptized (16:32-34). The verse is best understood as an invitation extended to an entire household present that night, followed immediately by the household actually hearing and responding to the message themselves — not a formula guaranteeing outcomes for family members apart from their own faith.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Acts 16:31 mean my family will be saved just because I believe?**
The passage itself shows the household hearing the word and believing directly (16:32-34), not being saved automatically through the jailer's faith alone. The offer of salvation extends to the household, but each person's response to the message is still part of the story.

**Why did the jailer almost kill himself before asking about salvation?**
Roman guards were held personally responsible, often with their own lives, for prisoners who escaped. Believing the earthquake had let the prisoners flee, he assumed he would face execution and prepared to take his own life before Paul stopped him by confirming everyone was still there.

**What happened to the jailer and his household after this verse?**
Acts 16:32-34 says Paul and Silas spoke the word of the Lord to the whole household, the jailer washed their wounds, he and his household were baptized that same night, and he set a meal before them, rejoicing that he had believed in God.
