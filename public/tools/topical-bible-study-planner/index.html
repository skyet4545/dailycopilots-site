      How to run a topical Bible study well

      A topical study is strongest when it lets each passage speak in context before gathering the theme together. Start broad, slow down around the anchor texts, and write one concrete application that follows from the passage rather than from a motivational slogan.

      1. Define the topic in plain language.
      2. Read the anchor passages in their surrounding chapters.
      3. Separate promises, commands, examples, warnings, and wisdom sayings.
      4. Compare the Old and New Testament witness.
      5. End with worship, repentance, obedience, or encouragement grounded in the text.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is a topical Bible study?

      A topical Bible study examines several passages that speak to one theme while still reading each passage in its own literary and historical context.

      How should I avoid taking verses out of context?

      Read each passage's surrounding paragraph or chapter, identify the original audience and argument, then compare the theme across Scripture before making an application.
