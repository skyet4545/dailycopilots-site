What Does Daniel 3:17-18 Mean? "But If Not"

📖 Bible Copilot ⏱ ~3 min read

Short answer: In Daniel 3:17–18, three men facing execution declare that God is able to save them — "but if not," they will still refuse to worship a false god. It is a model of faith that trusts God's power and submits to God's will, even without a guarantee of rescue. Their obedience did not depend on the outcome.

The context: the fiery furnace

King Nebuchadnezzar had set up a golden image and commanded everyone to bow before it or be thrown into a blazing furnace. Three Hebrew exiles — Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego — refused. Verses 17–18 are their answer to the king's threat, spoken calmly under sentence of death. The story then records that God met them in the fire and they walked out unharmed (Daniel 3:24–27) — but their faith was declared before they knew that.

What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: "If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up" (Daniel 3:17–18).

  • "our God... is able to deliver us" — They affirm God's power without hesitation. Their faith starts with confidence in what God can do.
  • "But if not" — The pivot of the whole passage. They allow for the possibility that God might not rescue them from the fire.
  • "we will not serve thy gods" — Their obedience is settled either way. Rescue or death, they will not bow.

This is sometimes called "but if not" faith. It refuses two errors: presuming God must rescue on demand, and doubting that He can. It holds power and surrender together.

Cross-references

  • Job 13:15 — "Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him" (KJV).
  • Habakkuk 3:17–18 — Rejoicing in God even if the crops fail.
  • Acts 5:29 — "We ought to obey God rather than men" (KJV).
  • Romans 8:38–39 — Nothing can separate us from God's love.

How to apply Daniel 3:17-18 today

Most of us will never face a literal furnace, but we face moments where doing right may cost us — a job, a relationship, approval. This passage models faith that obeys regardless of the outcome. It does not demand that God guarantee a happy ending before we trust Him. When you pray about a hard situation, "but if not" faith says: I believe You can, I will trust You if You do not, and either way I will not compromise. That posture frees you from bargaining with God.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "but if not" mean in Daniel 3? It is the phrase where the three men acknowledge God might not rescue them from the furnace — and affirm they will obey Him anyway. It expresses trust in God's will even without a guaranteed rescue.

Did God save Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego? Yes. Daniel 3 records that they were thrown into the fire but walked out unharmed, with a fourth figure seen with them in the flames (vv. 24–27). But they declared their faith before knowing they would be spared.

Isn't it a lack of faith to say "but if not"? No — it is deeper faith. They fully affirmed God's power to save, then submitted to His will regardless. True faith trusts God's character, not just a desired outcome, and does not make obedience conditional on rescue.
