What Does Deuteronomy 31:6 Mean? “Be Strong and Courageous”

By Bible Copilot Updated July 3, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards

Deuteronomy 31:6 tells Israel to face the transition into the promised land with courage because God would go with them and would not abandon them. The command was first given in a specific covenant and military setting. Its enduring principle is confidence rooted in God’s faithful presence, not confidence in human strength.

Understanding Deuteronomy 31:6 at Its Core

Deuteronomy 31:6 meaning encompasses far more than surface-level encouragement. The verse reads, "Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or terrified because of them, for the LORD your God goes with you; he will never leave you nor forsake you." When Moses speaks these words to Joshua and the Israelites at the threshold of Canaan, he's not merely offering motivational words. He's establishing a theological principle that becomes foundational to biblical faith. The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning rests on the absolute reliability of God's character. This isn't wishful thinking or false comfort; it's a promise grounded in covenant relationship. God doesn't promise the absence of enemies or obstacles—He promises His presence amid them. Understanding deuteronomy 31:6 meaning requires recognizing that courage isn't the absence of fear; it's moving forward despite fear, anchored to divine presence.

The Theological Depth: What Makes This Promise Remarkable

The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning reveals several theological truths that deserve careful examination. First, the verse establishes that courage is a divine gift, not a human achievement. When God commands strength and courage, He simultaneously provides the basis for both. The phrase "the LORD your God goes with you" isn't metaphorical reassurance—it's the ontological foundation of biblical faith. God's very presence becomes the source of human courage.

Second, the promise contains a negative affirmation: "he will never leave you nor forsake you." This double negative (in the original Hebrew, "lo yaphshitcha lo yashkecha") creates an emphatic denial of abandonment. God's presence isn't conditional, temporary, or dependent on human performance. It's absolute and eternal. Third, the context matters profoundly. Moses delivers this charge when he's about to leave the leadership to Joshua. The younger generation faces uncertainty, potential military defeat, and the overwhelming task of conquering Canaan. Yet within this vulnerability, God's promise stands unshaken.

The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning also encompasses the transition from liberation to conquest. The Exodus generation experienced God's miraculous power. Joshua's generation must exercise faith in that same God while facing new challenges. This principle extends to every believer's spiritual journey—we inherit promises made to previous generations of faith, and we're called to actualize those promises in our unique circumstances.

The Emotional and Psychological Dimension

Understanding deuteronomy 31:6 meaning includes recognizing the psychological accuracy of its language. The verse addresses fear at multiple levels. "Do not be afraid" (al tira) addresses emotional paralysis. "Do not be terrified" (al te'aratz) addresses the visceral, bodily response to threat. Biblical authors understood that fear isn't merely intellectual; it's embodied. The verse doesn't dismiss these reactions; instead, it provides a framework for moving beyond them.

The human experience of courage involves genuine psychological struggle. Joshua and the Israelites faced real enemies with real weapons. Their fear was rational. Yet the deuteronomy 31:6 meaning calls them to a higher rationality—one that accounts for God's presence and power. This creates a psychological reorientation where fear becomes one factor among many, rather than the dominant reality.

Modern psychology confirms this biblical insight. Courage isn't fearlessness; it's action in the face of legitimate fear. The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning aligns perfectly with this understanding. When God says "be strong and courageous," He's calling for a specific kind of psychological posture: acknowledgment of difficulty, yet refusal to be controlled by it.

Historical and Covenantal Context

The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning cannot be separated from the broader covenant narrative. This promise appears as Moses reaches the end of his life and ministry. Deuteronomy itself is structured as covenant renewal—Moses reminds Israel of their covenant with God, recounts their history, and calls them forward into their promised future. Within this framework, the promise of divine presence isn't arbitrary comfort; it's the reaffirmation of covenant loyalty.

The original audience needed this promise because they stood at a threshold. Everything familiar was changing. Moses, their irreplaceable leader, was stepping down. The wilderness wanderings were ending. Potential military conflict loomed. In such moments of transition, the deuteronomy 31:6 meaning becomes especially potent. God isn't merely saying, "Don't worry." He's saying, "Your circumstances are changing, but your relationship with me is unchanging."

The Relation to Subsequent Biblical Promises

The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning echoes throughout Scripture, creating a thematic thread that unifies biblical theology. Joshua 1:9 directly applies this promise: "Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged, for the LORD your God will be with you wherever you go." The duplication isn't accidental; Joshua needed this same assurance as he stepped into leadership. The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning proves transferable across different contexts and individuals.

Hebrews 13:5 makes an explicit connection: "Keep your lives free from the love of money and be content with what you have, because God has said, 'Never will I leave you; never will I forsake you.'" Here, the deuteronomy 31:6 meaning becomes applicable to financial anxiety and contentment. The principle extends beyond military courage into every dimension of life where fear tempts us toward faithlessness.

The Promise's Exclusivity and Inclusivity

A subtle but crucial aspect of deuteronomy 31:6 meaning involves understanding the scope of the promise. When God promises presence to Joshua and Israel, these are specific recipients of a specific covenant. Yet the New Testament applies this promise to all believers, suggesting that through Christ, we inherit the covenantal promises made to Israel. This theological move—from exclusive to inclusive—represents one of Scripture's most important developments.

The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning, therefore, operates at multiple levels. At its original level, it's God's promise to Joshua and the Israelites. At the level of Scripture's development, it becomes applicable to every believer in every age. This expansion doesn't diminish the original meaning; it validates and extends it. We don't claim promises that aren't ours, but through covenant participation in Christ, these promises become ours.

Practical Theology: Living Out the Meaning

Understanding deuteronomy 31:6 meaning theoretically must translate into lived experience. The promise isn't meant for intellectual contemplation alone; it's meant for embodiment in concrete circumstances. When a believer faces genuine danger, genuine loss, or genuine uncertainty, the deuteronomy 31:6 meaning offers not escape from difficulty but presence within it.

This creates a particular kind of faith—not the faith of someone who denies problems exist, but the faith of someone who acknowledges problems while trusting that God remains present and committed. The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning teaches that faith isn't about favorable circumstances; it's about sustained covenant relationship.

Questions That Deepen Understanding

Does "never leave nor forsake" mean nothing bad will happen to me? Not necessarily. The verse promises presence, not prevention of suffering. Many biblical figures experienced tremendous difficulty—imprisonment, exile, loss—while God was present with them.

How does this promise work if I don't feel God's presence? Feeling and presence are distinct. Emotions fluctuate; divine promises remain. The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning anchors to God's character, not our emotional state.

What if I'm facing something Joshua never faced? The deuteronomy 31:6 meaning applies because the principle transcends specific situations. Whatever your "Canaan conquest," the same God offers the same presence.
