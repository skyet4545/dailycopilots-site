What Does Psalm 143:8 Mean? Hearing God in the Morning

📖 Bible Copilot ⏱ ~3 min read

Short answer: Psalm 143:8 is a morning prayer. David asks God to let him "hear" His steadfast love at the start of the day and to show him which way to walk — because he has entrusted his whole life to God. It links a fresh sense of God's love each morning to clear direction for the day.

The context: a prayer from exhaustion

Psalm 143 is one of the traditional "penitential psalms." David is overwhelmed — verse 3 describes an enemy who has crushed his life to the ground, and verse 4 admits his spirit is faint and his heart is desolate. From that low place he keeps reaching for God's mercy. Verse 8 is where the psalm pivots from despair toward hope: he asks for a fresh morning word of love and guidance.

What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: "Cause me to hear thy lovingkindness in the morning; for in thee do I trust: cause me to know the way wherein I should walk; for I lift up my soul unto thee" (Psalm 143:8).

  • "Cause me to hear thy lovingkindness in the morning" — "Lovingkindness" translates the rich Hebrew word chesed, God's loyal covenant love. David wants the day to begin with a reminder of that love, not with dread.
  • "for in thee do I trust" — His request rests on relationship, not merit. He is leaning on God because he has already committed to trusting Him.
  • "cause me to know the way wherein I should walk" — He wants direction, a clear path for real decisions.
  • "for I lift up my soul unto thee" — To "lift up the soul" pictures surrender and longing, offering his whole inner life to God.

Notice the order: love first, then guidance. David asks to be reassured of God's love before he asks which way to go — because it is easier to follow direction from someone you know loves you.

Cross-references

  • Lamentations 3:22–23 — God's mercies "are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness" (KJV).
  • Psalm 5:3 — "My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee" (KJV).
  • Proverbs 3:5–6 — Trust in the LORD and "he shall direct thy paths" (KJV).
  • Isaiah 30:21 — "This is the way, walk ye in it" (KJV).

How to apply Psalm 143:8 today

This verse is almost designed to be prayed at daybreak. Before the noise of the day, ask God for two things in order: a renewed sense of His love, and direction for the decisions ahead. Many believers make it a habit to open the morning by reading it slowly and personalizing it. Starting the day anchored in God's chesed changes how you carry its pressures.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does David ask to "hear" God's lovingkindness? In Hebrew, hearing often implies receiving and responding, not just sound. David wants a felt, fresh assurance of God's love to reach him — something he can rest on as the day begins.

Is Psalm 143:8 specifically a morning prayer? The verse names the morning directly, which is why it is often used as a daily starting prayer. But its heart — asking for God's love and guidance from a weary place — fits any moment you need both.

What does "lift up my soul" mean? It is a picture of offering your whole self to God in surrender and longing. David is not just asking for help; he is placing his entire inner life in God's hands as he seeks direction.
