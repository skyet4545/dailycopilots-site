What God Says About Money: A Scripture-Based Guide

By Bible Copilot Updated July 3, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards
Quick answer

Discover what God says about money through Scripture. Learn biblical principles on wealth, generosity, and financial stewardship for spiritual growth.

Introduction

What God says about money directly addresses one of life's most pressing concerns. Throughout Scripture, from the laws of Moses to the teachings of Jesus to the epistles of Paul, what God says about money reveals His perspective on resources, stewardship, and the human heart. Understanding what God says about money transforms your financial life from one of anxiety and materialism to one of purpose and peace.

What God says about money isn't primarily a condemnation of wealth or a guarantee of poverty. Rather, what God says about money provides a framework for understanding your relationship with resources. What God says about money addresses money's power to shape your heart, influence your decisions, and define your priorities.

What God Says About Money Through Jesus

What God says about money is perhaps most powerfully revealed through Jesus's teaching. Jesus spoke about money more frequently than almost any other topic. Matthew 6:24 contains one of Jesus's most direct statements: "No one can serve two masters. Either you will hate the one and love the other, or you will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve both God and money."

What God says about money through Jesus is that divided loyalty is impossible. You cannot partially serve both God and wealth. What God says about money demands total allegiance to God. Jesus taught this because He understood that money's pull on the human heart is powerful and exclusive.

What God says about money through Jesus's parables reinforces this teaching. The parable of the talents (Matthew 25:14-30) shows that God expects faithful stewardship. The parable of the rich fool (Luke 12:15-21) warns against trusting wealth. What God says about money through the parable of the sower (Mark 4:19) identifies "the worries of this life and the deceitfulness of wealth" as thorns choking spiritual growth.

What God Says About Money Through Paul

What God says about money comes also through Paul's epistles. 1 Timothy 6:9 records Paul's warning: "People who want to get rich fall into temptation and a trap and into many foolish and harmful desires that plunge men into ruin and destruction."

What God says about money through Paul isn't anti-wealth but anti-greed. 1 Timothy 6:17-19 addresses the wealthy: "Command those who are rich in this present world not to be arrogant nor to put their hope in wealth, which is so uncertain, but to put their hope in God, who richly provides us with everything for our enjoyment. Command them to do good, to be rich in good deeds, and to be generous and willing to share."

What God says about money through Paul emphasizes generosity as the remedy for wealth's spiritual dangers. 2 Corinthians 9:6-7 teaches, "Whoever sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and whoever sows generously will also reap generously. Each of you should give what you have decided in your heart to give, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver."

What God Says About Money Through Proverbs

What God says about money comes also through the wisdom literature. Proverbs contains abundant guidance about financial matters. Proverbs 10:4 states, "Lazy hands make for poverty, but diligent hands bring wealth." What God says about money affirms that work produces provision.

What God says about money through Proverbs includes warnings about greed. Proverbs 15:27 warns, "The greedy bring ruin to their households." What God says about money teaches that greed destroys families and communities. Proverbs 22:7 reveals debt's power: "The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender."

What God says about money through Proverbs 27:23-24 encourages planning: "Be sure you know the condition of your flocks, for your cattle do not last forever. When the hay is gone and new growth appears and the grass from the hills is gathered in, the lambs will provide you with clothing, and the goats with the price of a field."

What God Says About Money About Stewardship

What God says about money begins with stewardship. Psalm 24:1 establishes, "The earth is the Lord's, and everything in it, the world, and all who live in it." What God says about money is that you own nothing. Everything belongs to God, and you're a manager of what He entrusts.

1 Peter 4:10 reinforces this: "Each of you should use whatever gift you have received to serve others, as faithful stewards of God's grace in its various forms." What God says about money means every financial decision is an act of stewardship with spiritual significance.

What God says about money teaches that stewardship includes accountability. You'll answer to God for how you used the resources entrusted to you. This framework transforms financial decisions from merely personal optimization to spiritual responsibility.

What God Says About Money About Generosity

What God says about money emphasizes generosity as a defining spiritual practice. Luke 6:38 records Jesus's promise: "Give, and it will be given to you. A good measure, pressed down, shaken together and running over, will be poured into your lap." What God says about money suggests that generosity produces blessing.

What God says about money through the early church in Acts 2:44-45 shows believers sharing possessions to meet needs. What God says about money through this model suggests radical generosity as appropriate Christian practice, at least for those with means to help.

What God says about money through Proverbs 11:25 teaches, "A generous person will prosper; whoever refreshes others will be refreshed." What God says about money connects personal flourishing with generosity toward others.

What God Says About Money About Honesty

What God says about money demands honesty in all financial dealings. Proverbs 10:2 warns, "Ill-gotten treasures bring no benefit, but righteousness delivers from death." What God says about money teaches that dishonest gain is ultimately worthless.

What God says about money in the Ten Commandments includes "You shall not steal" (Exodus 20:15) and "You shall not covet your neighbor's possessions" (Exodus 20:17). What God says about money about honesty extends to both external behavior and internal attitudes.

What God says about money through Proverbs 13:11 affirms patience: "Dishonest money dwindles away, but whoever gathers money little by little makes it grow." What God says about money values slow, steady, honest accumulation over quick, questionable gains.

What God Says About Money About Contentment

What God says about money includes the liberating principle of contentment. 1 Timothy 6:6-8 teaches, "But godliness with contentment is great gain. For we brought nothing into the world, and we can take nothing out of it. But if we have food and clothing, we will be content with that."

What God says about money suggests that wanting less brings more happiness than having more. Hebrews 13:5 promises, "Keep your lives free from the love of money and be content with what you have, because God has said, 'Never will I leave you; never will I forsake you.'"

What God says about money about contentment isn't settling for deprivation but cultivating grateful acceptance of your current provision. This mindset breaks the endless comparison and competition culture promotes.

What God Says About Money About Trust

What God says about money includes calls for radical trust. Matthew 6:31-33 records Jesus's teaching: "So do not worry, saying, 'What shall we eat?' or 'What shall we drink?' or 'What shall we wear?' For the pagans run after all these things, and your Father knows that you need them. But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well."

What God says about money isn't that planning is wrong but that anxious obsession is. What God says about money calls believers to prioritize God's kingdom while trusting God's provision. Philippians 4:19 promises, "And my God will meet all your needs according to the riches of his glory in Christ Jesus."

FAQ

Q: What God says about money—is it anti-wealth? A: No. What God says about money celebrates honest wealth and wealthy godly people. What God says about money opposes greed, dishonesty, and trusting wealth instead of God, not wealth itself.

Q: Does what God says about money require me to give away all my possessions? A: Not universally. What God says about money calls for generosity and willingness to share. For some individuals, radical simplicity is called; for others, stewardship of abundance is the calling. What God says about money demands detachment from possessions, not necessarily poverty.

Q: How does what God says about money apply to paying taxes? A: Romans 13:7 teaches, "Give to everyone what you owe them: If you owe taxes, pay taxes." What God says about money requires honoring legitimate civic obligations.

Q: What God says about money—does it address retirement savings? A: What God says about money through Proverbs 21:5 affirms planning. Prudent saving for retirement's legitimate needs demonstrates wise stewardship.

Q: Does what God says about money mean I shouldn't pursue career advancement? A: No. What God says about money celebrates diligent work. The question is whether you pursue advancement with integrity and whether success replaces God as your source of identity and security.
