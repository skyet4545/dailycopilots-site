Deuteronomy 6:5 Meaning: Loving God with Heart, Soul and Might

By Bible Copilot Updated October 2, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards

Quick answer

Deuteronomy 6:5 calls Israel to love the Lord with all the heart, soul and might. The repeated “all” emphasizes wholehearted devotion. Verses 6–9 carry that command into daily life through remembering, teaching and speaking about God’s words. The passage is more than an instruction to produce a strong religious feeling.

Start with Hear, Israel

The address “Hear, Israel” begins verse 4, immediately before the command to love in verse 5. The following verses speak of keeping these words on the heart and teaching them to children. Reading verses 4–9 together preserves the relationship between the confession about God, the command to love and its daily expression.

Why heart, soul and might are named together

Notice the repeated word “all” before dividing the terms into separate categories. The verse joins them in a single command. A useful first reading is to ask what wholehearted love would involve, rather than treating the phrase as a technical diagram that assigns each word one exclusive part of human life.

How the surrounding verses make love concrete

Verse 7 names ordinary settings: at home, on the way, lying down and rising. Verse 8 names signs on the hand and between the eyes; verse 9 names doorposts and gates. These details make the words present through daily life. They should not be collapsed into a vague instruction to think about God occasionally.

A simple devotional exercise

Choose one ordinary moment already in your day, such as getting up or sitting at home. Read verses 4–9, then write one sentence about what you noticed and one question you still have. If you study with someone else, discuss the question together. This is a suggested response to the passage, not a new rule or a measure of someone else’s faith.

Three questions help trace the passage:

  1. How does verse 4 prepare for the command in verse 5?
  2. What does repeating “all” emphasize?
  3. Which daily settings in verses 6–9 help you see how remembering and teaching belong together?
