Top 25 Bible Verses About Gossip (With Explanations)

By Bible Copilot Updated July 3, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards
Quick answer

Comprehensive list of 25 Bible verses about gossip with explanations and applications for understanding scriptural teaching on harmful speech.

Introduction

The Bible contains numerous verses about gossip spread throughout both testaments. These Bible verses about gossip provide comprehensive guidance for anyone seeking to understand God's perspective on this destructive habit. The Bible verses about gossip range from direct commands to avoid it, to descriptions of its damage, to teachings about guarding our speech. This collection of Bible verses about gossip shows how consistently Scripture addresses this issue and how seriously God takes our words. Whether you're studying this topic for personal growth, to address problems in your community, or to better understand biblical principles, these Bible verses about gossip will deepen your understanding.

Top 25 Bible Verses About Gossip With Explanations

1. Proverbs 20:19 (ESV)

"Whoever goes about slandering reveals secrets; therefore do not associate with a babbler." This verse defines gossip as revealing secrets and advises avoiding those who gossip habitually.

2. Proverbs 16:28 (ESV)

"A dishonest man spreads strife, and a whisperer separates close friends." This verse shows that gossip actively damages relationships between others, not just the person being discussed.

3. Leviticus 19:16 (ESV)

"You shall not go around as a slanderer among your people, and you shall not stand up against the life of your neighbor." God's direct law prohibiting slander as part of His foundational commands for His people.

4. Proverbs 11:13 (ESV)

"A gossip betrays a confidence, but a trustworthy person keeps a secret." This verse identifies gossip fundamentally as a betrayal of trust that someone has placed in you.

5. 1 Timothy 5:13 (ESV)

"Besides that, they learn to be idlers, going about from house to house, and not only idlers but also gossips and busybodies, saying what they should not." Paul connects gossip with idleness and lack of purpose, suggesting gossip becomes a default activity.

6. Proverbs 18:8 (ESV)

"The words of a gossip are like choice morsels; they go down to the inmost parts of a person." This verse suggests gossip becomes psychologically addictive, feeding our desire for drama and intrigue.

7. 1 Peter 2:1 (ESV)

"So put away all malice and all deceit and hypocrisy and envy and all slander." Peter calls believers to put away slander as part of maturing spiritually.

8. James 3:5-6 (ESV)

"Consider what a great forest is set on fire by a small spark. The tongue also is a fire, a world of evil among the parts of your body." James uses powerful imagery showing how the tongue spreads destruction rapidly like wildfire.

9. Ephesians 4:29-30 (ESV)

"Let no corrupting talk come out of your mouths, but only such as is good for building up, as fits the occasion, that it may give grace to those who hear...And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God." Paul teaches that all speech should build up, and that gossip grieves the Holy Spirit.

10. Romans 1:29-30 (ESV)

"They were filled with all manner of unrighteousness, evil, covetousness, malice. They are full of envy, murder, strife, deceit, maliciousness. They are gossips, slanderers, haters of God." Gossip is listed among serious sins characterizing those who reject God.

11. Proverbs 26:20-22 (ESV)

"For lack of wood the fire goes out; and where there is no whisperer, quarreling ceases. As charcoal to hot embers and wood to fire, so is a quarrelsome person to kindle strife." This verse shows that gossip fuels conflict and that eliminating gossip reduces quarreling.

12. 1 Corinthians 12:25-26 (ESV)

"But God has so composed the body, giving greater honor to the part that lacked it, that there may be no division in the body, but that the members may have the same care for one another." The principle of equal concern in the body of Christ contradicts gossip's tendency to create hierarchies.

13. Matthew 12:34 (ESV)

"For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks." Jesus teaches that speech reflects what's in the heart, so addressing gossip requires addressing the heart condition.

14. Matthew 7:1-2 (ESV)

"Judge not, that you be not judged. For with the judgment you pronounce you will be judged." Jesus warns that judging others (which gossip often does) results in being judged ourselves.

15. Luke 6:37 (ESV)

"Judge not, and you will not be judged; condemn not, and you will not be condemned; forgive, and you will be forgiven." The contrast between judgment/condemnation and forgiveness shows gossip opposes the grace Jesus teaches.

16. Proverbs 15:4 (ESV)

"The tongue that brings healing is a tree of life, but a deceitful tongue crushes the spirit." This verse contrasts healing speech with destructive speech, showing our words either give life or crush others.

17. Proverbs 13:3 (ESV)

"Whoever guards his mouth preserves his life; he who opens wide his lips comes to ruin." Restraint in speech leads to preservation; uncontrolled speech leads to ruin.

18. Proverbs 21:23 (ESV)

"Whoever keeps his mouth and his tongue keeps himself out of trouble." Guarding what we say protects us from serious consequences.

19. Psalm 34:13 (ESV)

"Keep your tongue from evil and your lips from speaking lies." Even in the Psalms, guarding speech from evil is presented as a spiritual discipline.

20. Psalm 15:1-3 (ESV)

"O Lord, who shall sojourn in your tent? Who shall dwell on your holy hill? He who walks blamelessly and does what is right and speaks truth in his heart; who does not slander with his tongue." Dwelling in God's presence requires not slandering others with our tongue.

21. Proverbs 10:19 (ESV)

"When words are many, sin is not absent, but he who holds his tongue is wise." More words increase the likelihood of sin (including gossip); restraint is wisdom.

22. 1 Thessalonians 5:11 (ESV)

"Therefore encourage one another and build each other up, just as in fact you are doing." Paul calls believers to use speech to encourage and build, the opposite of gossip.

23. 2 Corinthians 12:20 (ESV)

"For I fear that when I come I may not find you as I wish, and you may not find me as you wish. I fear that there may be quarreling, jealousy, outbursts of anger, factions, slander, gossip, arrogance and disorder." Paul's concern about gossip in the church shows it was an ongoing problem in early Christian communities.

24. Proverbs 27:12 (ESV)

"The prudent see danger and take refuge, but the simple keep going and pay the penalty." Prudence includes recognizing the danger of gossip and taking action to avoid it.

25. 1 Peter 4:4 (ESV)

"They are surprised that you do not join them in the same flood of debauchery, and they malign you." When we refuse to participate in gossip and other destructive behaviors, some will speak against us for it.

FAQ

Q: Why does the Bible have so many verses about gossip? A: Gossip is destructive and common. The Bible's repeated warnings show how serious God takes this issue and how prevalent it is even in faithful communities.

Q: Are all these verses about gossip specifically, or some about broader speech issues? A: Some address gossip specifically, others address slander, and some address speech and tongue control more broadly. All are relevant to understanding the Bible's teaching on gossip.

Q: Which verse about gossip is most important? A: They're all important, but Proverbs 20:19 provides a clear definition, and Ephesians 4:29-30 explains the spiritual impact. Together they show what gossip is and why it matters.

Q: How many times does the Bible mention gossip? A: Dozens of times when counting direct references to gossip, slander, and related tongue issues. The frequency shows this is a major biblical concern.

Q: What's the most practical Bible verse about gossip? A: Many are practical, but Proverbs 20:19's advice to avoid associating with gossips provides clear, actionable guidance.
