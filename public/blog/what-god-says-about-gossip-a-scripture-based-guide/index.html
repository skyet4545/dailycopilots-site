What God Says About Gossip: A Scripture-Based Guide

By Bible Copilot Updated July 3, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards
Quick answer

Learn what God specifically says about gossip through Scripture with direct Bible verses and guidance for Christians who want to honor God through their speech.

Introduction

What God says about gossip should matter deeply to every Christian. Our communication directly reflects our relationship with God and our values as believers. What God says about gossip goes beyond surface prohibitions to reveal His heart for community, integrity, and the power of words. Throughout Scripture, what God says about gossip emerges as a consistent, serious teaching rooted in His character and His concern for His people. When you understand what God says about gossip, you gain not just rules to follow but principles that transform how you relate to others. This comprehensive guide explores what God says about gossip through His Word and shows how His teaching applies to your life today.

What God Says About Gossip in His Law

What God says about gossip begins in His foundational laws for His people. Leviticus 19:16 contains God's direct command: "Do not go around as a slanderer among your people." Notice that what God says about gossip here is stated as a command from God Himself, not a suggestion from humans. God personally cares about how His people speak about one another.

The context of Leviticus 19 shows what God says about gossip in relation to other commands. This chapter addresses sexuality, commerce, treatment of servants, and relationships. What God says about gossip grouped with these fundamental ethical issues shows that God sees speech integrity as central to righteous living.

Exodus 23:1 adds to what God says about gossip: "Do not spread false reports." While this specifically addresses false reports, it connects gossip with the broader issue of false speech. What God says about gossip includes commitment to truthfulness and integrity.

What God Says About Gossip in Wisdom Literature

The book of Proverbs reveals what God says about gossip through practical wisdom. Proverbs 11:13 states: "A gossip betrays a confidence, but a trustworthy person keeps a secret." What God says about gossip emphasizes that when we spread someone's secrets, we betray their trust fundamentally.

What God says about gossip is reinforced in Proverbs 16:28: "A perverse person stirs up conflict and separates close friends." Notice that what God says about gossip addresses not just harm to the person being discussed but harm to the entire network of relationships.

Proverbs 20:19 warns: "Whoever goes about slandering reveals secrets; therefore do not associate with a babbler." This teaches what God says about gossip includes the reality that associating with habitual gossips puts us at risk. What God says about gossip suggests we protect ourselves by distancing from those who spread rumors.

Proverbs 18:8 describes what God says about gossip in memorable terms: "The words of a gossip are like choice morsels; they go down to the inmost parts." What God says about gossip suggests it becomes psychologically addictive—we crave the drama and intrigue of spreading rumors.

What God Says About Gossip Through His Prophets

The prophets reinforced what God says about gossip. Jeremiah frequently addressed the issue of false speech and slander. Jeremiah 9:4 expresses God's frustration about false speech and gossip within His people. What God says about gossip through Jeremiah shows His exasperation with a people who refuse to honor integrity in speech.

What God says about gossip appears in Habakkuk as well, where what God says about gossip includes judgment against those who spread lies and slander.

What God Says About Gossip in the New Testament

What God says about gossip continues with equal intensity in the New Testament. Romans 1:29-30 includes what God says about gossip in Paul's description of people who have rejected God: they are "filled with every kind of wickedness, evil, greed and depravity. They are full of envy, murder, strife, deceit and malice. They are gossips, slanderers, God-haters." What God says about gossip by placing it with such grave sins shows the seriousness of the offense.

What God says about gossip appears again in 1 Corinthians, where Paul addresses church issues. What God says about gossip includes concern for how it damages the body of Christ.

1 Timothy 5:13 shows what God says about gossip in Paul's instructions to churches about managing different groups. What God says about gossip associates it with idleness and busybodies who have no meaningful purpose.

What God Says About Gossip Through Jesus

What God says about gossip reaches its fullest expression in Jesus's teaching about the tongue and speech. In Matthew 12:34, Jesus teaches: "For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks." This is what God says about gossip—it's not merely a behavioral problem but a heart condition.

What God says about gossip includes Jesus's principle in Matthew 7:1-2 about judging others. "Do not judge, or you too will be judged. For in the same way you judge others, you will be judged." What God says about gossip includes this reality: when we judge and condemn others through gossip, we position ourselves for judgment.

What God says about gossip is also reflected in Luke 6:37: "Do not judge, and you will not be judged. Do not condemn, and you will not be condemned. Forgive, and you will be forgiven." What God says about gossip through Jesus emphasizes grace and forgiveness rather than judgment and condemnation.

What God Says About Gossip Regarding Our Purposes

Ephesians 4:29 summarizes what God says about gossip in practical terms: "Do not let any unwholesome talk come out of your mouths, but only what is helpful for building others up according to their needs, that it may benefit those who listen." What God says about gossip here provides a positive standard: our speech should build others up.

What God says about gossip extends to 1 Thessalonians 5:11: "Therefore encourage one another and build each other up, just as in fact you are doing." What God says about gossip implies that we should be using our words to encourage, not to tear down.

What God Says About Gossip and Community

What God says about gossip includes its impact on community. The principle in 1 Corinthians 12:25-26 about the body of Christ applies: we should have equal concern for all members. What God says about gossip contradicts this by creating in-groups and out-groups, elevating some while diminishing others.

What God says about gossip matters for the church's witness. When Christians are known for gossip rather than integrity, the gospel's credibility suffers. What God says about gossip includes the reality that our speech affects how others perceive our faith.

FAQ

Q: What God says about gossip—is it ever appropriate to share someone's personal information? A: What God says about gossip distinguishes between sharing information with permission for a legitimate purpose and spreading it without permission. Permission and purpose matter.

Q: According to what God says about gossip, is discussing someone's public failure gossip? A: What God says about gossip focuses on motivation and context. Discussing public information for legitimate reasons differs from spreading it to entertain or harm.

Q: What God says about gossip—how do I respond when someone tries to gossip to me? A: What God says about gossip suggests refusing to participate. Politely say you're uncomfortable with that conversation or suggest they speak directly to the person.

Q: What God says about gossip—should I confront someone who's gossiping about me? A: What God says about gossip supports direct conversation when possible. Go to them with humility and seek understanding and reconciliation.

Q: What God says about gossip—can Christians ever discuss others' behaviors or problems? A: What God says about gossip allows for discussing something to understand it or address it appropriately, but not to entertain or harm.
