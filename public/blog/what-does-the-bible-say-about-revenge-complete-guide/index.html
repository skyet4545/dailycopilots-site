What Does the Bible Say About Revenge? (Complete Guide)

By Bible Copilot Updated July 3, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards
Quick answer

Discover what the Bible says about revenge with this complete guide. Learn biblical principles on justice, forgiveness, and trusting God with wrongs.

Many Christians wonder what the Bible says about revenge when they've been deeply wronged. The question of what the Bible says about revenge is crucial because answering it correctly can free you from destructive cycles of retaliation. Understanding what the Bible says about revenge requires looking at multiple passages that address this human impulse from different angles. This complete guide will show you exactly what the Bible says about revenge and how these teachings apply to your situation.

What the Bible Says About Revenge: The Direct Command

Romans 12:19 is clear about what the Bible says about revenge: "Do not take revenge, my dear friends, but leave room for God's wrath, for it is written: 'It is mine to avenge; I will repay,' says the Lord." This is foundational to what the Bible says about revenge.

What the Bible says about revenge here is unambiguous: don't do it. But what makes this more than a prohibition is what the Bible says about revenge continues with a reason: God has already claimed justice as His responsibility. What the Bible says about revenge teaches that we don't have to carry the burden of ensuring fairness.

What the Bible Says About Revenge: The Alternative Response

Matthew 5:38-40 shows what the Bible says about revenge in terms of alternatives: "You have heard that it was said, 'Eye for eye, and tooth for tooth.' But I tell you, do not resist an evil person. If someone strikes you on the right cheek, turn to him the other also. And if someone wants to sue you and take your shirt, give him your cloak as well."

What the Bible says about revenge here supersedes Old Testament "eye for eye" justice. What the Bible says about revenge, according to Jesus, is to respond with unexpected grace. This is shocking—what the Bible says about revenge calls for the opposite of retaliation.

What the Bible Says About Revenge: Through Christ's Example

1 Peter 2:23 demonstrates what the Bible says about revenge through Jesus: "When they hurled their insults at him, he did not retaliate; when he suffered, he made no threats. Instead, he entrusted himself to him who judges justly." What the Bible says about revenge is modeled by Christ Himself.

What the Bible says about revenge shows that even when unjustly treated, Jesus didn't pursue revenge. What the Bible says about revenge through Christ's example teaches that trusting God's justice is possible, even in extreme circumstances.

What the Bible Says About Revenge: Trust in God's Justice

Hebrews 10:30 reinforces what the Bible says about revenge about God's promise: "For we know him who said, 'It is mine to avenge; I will repay.'" What the Bible says about revenge assures us that justice will happen.

What the Bible says about revenge teaches that God takes wrongdoing seriously. He promises to repay. What the Bible says about revenge allows us to release our need for revenge because we trust God's justice more than we trust our own sense of fairness.

What the Bible Says About Revenge: The Cycle of Harm

Proverbs 27:12 explains what the Bible says about revenge in terms of consequences: "The prudent see danger and take refuge, but the simple keep going and pay the penalty." What the Bible says about revenge shows that pursuing it leads to harm.

What the Bible says about revenge teaches that revenge typically initiates cycles of counterrevenge. What the Bible says about revenge warns that the wise recognize this danger and avoid it.

What the Bible Says About Revenge: Overcome Evil With Good

Romans 12:21 shows what the Bible says about revenge offers as an alternative: "Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good." What the Bible says about revenge isn't just negative—it's positive.

What the Bible says about revenge teaches that responding with good actually breaks cycles of harm. What the Bible says about revenge recognizes that true victory comes through choosing good, not through retaliation.

What the Bible Says About Revenge: The Role of Prayer

Matthew 5:44 reveals what the Bible says about revenge in terms of prayer: "Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you." What the Bible says about revenge includes this practical mechanism for releasing the desire for retaliation.

What the Bible says about revenge shows that prayer changes us. When we genuinely pray for someone's wellbeing, what the Bible says about revenge makes clear that we can't simultaneously harbor revenge fantasies.

What the Bible Says About Revenge: Forgiveness as Freedom

Colossians 3:13 demonstrates what the Bible says about revenge through forgiveness: "Forgive as the Lord forgave you." What the Bible says about revenge connects to the forgiveness we've received.

What the Bible says about revenge teaches that seeking revenge contradicts the grace we've experienced. What the Bible says about revenge shows that forgiveness—releasing the person to God—is how we move toward peace.

What the Bible Says About Revenge: Letting Go of Bitterness

Ephesians 4:31 shows what the Bible says about revenge in relation to bitterness: "Let all bitterness, rage and anger, brawling and slander, and every form of malice be put away from you." What the Bible says about revenge addresses the emotional states that fuel retaliation.

What the Bible says about revenge teaches that we must remove the bitterness that desires revenge. What the Bible says about revenge calls us to actively eliminate these destructive emotions.

FAQ

Q: Does what the Bible says about revenge include getting justice? A: What the Bible says about revenge forbids personal revenge but allows pursuing justice through proper legal authorities and systems.

Q: According to what the Bible says about revenge, what if I haven't done anything wrong? A: What the Bible says about revenge applies especially to the innocent. Jesus was innocent yet chose not to seek revenge.

Q: What does the Bible say about revenge if someone keeps hurting people? A: What the Bible says about revenge doesn't forbid protecting yourself or seeking help from authorities. It forbids personal retaliation.

Q: How does what the Bible says about revenge address my anger? A: What the Bible says about revenge acknowledges that anger is a real response to injustice. The question is what you do with that anger—whether you pursue revenge or trust God.

Q: What's the first step in following what the Bible says about revenge? A: What the Bible says about revenge begins with deciding to release the person to God's justice. Ask God for grace to forgive and to let go of revenge fantasies.
