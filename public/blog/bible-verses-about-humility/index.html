Bible Verses About Humility: What Scripture Teaches

📖 Bible Copilot ⏱ ~3 min read

Short answer: The Bible presents humility as a defining mark of a godly life — a realistic, God-centered view of oneself that produces gratitude, service, and dependence on God rather than self-promotion. Scripture repeatedly says God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble, and it points to Jesus as the ultimate model of humility.

What the Bible teaches about humility

Biblical humility isn't thinking poorly of yourself or pretending to have no worth. It's seeing yourself truthfully before God — created, dependent, and loved — and letting that shape how you treat others. Several themes stand out.

God gives grace to the humble. This is one of the clearest patterns in Scripture.

  • James 4:6 — "God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble" (quoting Proverbs 3:34).
  • 1 Peter 5:5–6 — clothe yourselves with humility, "for God resisteth the proud... Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God."
  • Proverbs 11:2 — "with the lowly is wisdom."

Humility comes before honor; pride before a fall.

  • Proverbs 22:4 — "By humility and the fear of the LORD are riches, and honour, and life."
  • Proverbs 16:18 — "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall."
  • Matthew 23:12 — "Whosoever shall exalt himself shall be abased; and he that shall humble himself shall be exalted."

Humility shows in how we treat others.

  • Philippians 2:3 — "in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves."
  • Romans 12:16 — "be not wise in your own conceits."

God Himself defines the humble life.

  • Micah 6:8 — to "do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God."

Jesus as the model of humility

The New Testament roots humility in the example of Christ. Philippians 2:5–8 describes how Jesus, though equal with God, "made himself of no reputation," took the form of a servant, and humbled Himself even to death on a cross. Jesus said of Himself, "I am meek and lowly in heart" (Matthew 11:29), and He washed His disciples' feet as an example (John 13:14–15). Christian humility isn't merely a personality trait; it's following the pattern of Jesus.

Cross-references

  • Proverbs 3:34 — the source quoted by both James and Peter.
  • Isaiah 66:2 — God looks to the one "poor and of a contrite spirit."
  • Luke 14:11 — the same exalting-humbling principle in a parable.
  • Colossians 3:12 — put on "humbleness of mind."

How to apply it today

Humility grows in small, practical ways: giving others credit, listening before speaking, admitting when you're wrong, serving without being noticed, and receiving correction. Above all, Scripture frames humility as dependence on God — regularly acknowledging that what you have is a gift and that you need His grace. The promise attached is striking: God draws near to the humble and lifts them up in His time (1 Peter 5:6).

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the Bible mean by humility? Biblical humility is a truthful, God-centered view of oneself — recognizing dependence on God and valuing others — rather than self-deprecation. It expresses itself in service, gratitude, and teachability, as seen in passages like Philippians 2:3 and Micah 6:8.

Why does the Bible say God opposes the proud? Pride sets a person against dependence on God and often against others, which Scripture consistently identifies as the root of downfall (Proverbs 16:18). By contrast, God "giveth grace unto the humble" (James 4:6), because humility is open to His help.

How is Jesus an example of humility? Philippians 2:5–8 says Jesus, though equal with God, took the form of a servant and humbled Himself to the point of death on a cross. He also washed His disciples' feet and called Himself "meek and lowly in heart" (Matthew 11:29), modeling the humility He calls His followers to.
