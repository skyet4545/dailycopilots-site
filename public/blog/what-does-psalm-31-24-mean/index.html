What Does Psalm 31:24 Mean? Courage for the Hopeful

📖 Bible Copilot ⏱ ~4 min read

Short answer: Psalm 31:24 is David's closing charge to fellow believers after a psalm of intense distress: "Be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart, all ye that hope in the LORD" (KJV). Courage is commanded, strength is promised, and the promise is addressed specifically to those whose hope is set on God — not on their circumstances improving.

The context: a psalm that ends better than it began

Psalm 31 is one of David's rawest laments. He speaks of being in trouble, of his strength failing, of being forgotten "as a dead man out of mind" (v. 12, KJV), of slander and plotting against him. He is not describing a mild bad week.

But the psalm keeps returning to trust. Verse 5 contains words Jesus Himself quoted from the cross: "Into thine hand I commit my spirit" (compare Luke 23:46). Verse 15 says, "My times are in thy hand." By verses 19–23 David is praising God's goodness "laid up for them that fear thee" and urging God's people to love the LORD.

Verse 24 is the final line — a benediction turned outward. Having walked through his own fear and found God faithful, David turns to the congregation and tells them what he learned.

What it means, phrase by phrase

"Be of good courage." This is an imperative. Courage here is not a feeling that arrives; it is a stance you take. Scripture regularly commands courage (Joshua 1:9; 1 Corinthians 16:13), which implies courage is a choice made in the presence of fear, not the absence of it.

"And he shall strengthen your heart." The promise follows the command, not the other way around. God meets the step of courage with inner reinforcement. "Heart" in Hebrew usage covers mind, will, and resolve — the whole inner person that fear destabilizes.

"All ye that hope in the LORD." This clause defines the audience. The promise is not a generic pep talk for anyone who tries hard; it is for those who have placed their expectation on God. Hope in Scripture is not wishful thinking but settled confidence in God's character and promises.

Note the near-identical wording of Psalm 27:14. David appears to have carried this line with him. Repeated truth in the Psalter is a signal, not padding — some things need saying twice.

Cross-references

  • Psalm 27:14 — "Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart."
  • Joshua 1:9 — be strong and of a good courage; the LORD is with you wherever you go.
  • Isaiah 41:10 — fear not, I will strengthen thee, yea, I will help thee.
  • Psalm 31:15 — "My times are in thy hand."
  • Romans 15:13 — the God of hope filling you with joy and peace in believing.
  • 2 Corinthians 4:16 — the inward man renewed day by day.

How to apply it today

Psalm 31:24 gives an order of operations that runs against instinct. We usually want to feel strengthened first and then act bravely. David reverses it: take courage, and God strengthens the heart. The strengthening comes in the doing, not before it.

That means the application is concrete. Make the phone call. Go to the appointment. Keep showing up at church when you feel hollow. Tell the truth you've been avoiding. Courage in Scripture is almost always attached to a next step, and the promise of strength attaches to people already moving.

The qualifier also protects against a shallow reading. If your hope is in the outcome — the test result, the offer, the person changing — this verse is not addressed to you, and courage will keep collapsing every time the outcome wobbles. Hope anchored in the LORD survives outcomes going the wrong way, because its object hasn't moved.

One honest caution: this is a psalm, and psalms give us language for the whole range of faith, including despair. David commands courage in verse 24 after admitting in verse 22, "I said in my haste, I am cut off from before thine eyes" (KJV). If you are in the verse-22 place, you are still inside this psalm. The last line is where it goes, not the price of admission.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Psalm 31:24 different from Psalm 27:14? They are nearly the same sentence. Psalm 27:14 addresses the individual ("thine heart") and repeats "wait on the LORD"; Psalm 31:24 addresses the whole congregation ("your heart, all ye") and specifies "all ye that hope in the LORD." The emphasis in 31:24 falls on courage for a community, closing a psalm about personal distress with a word for everyone.

Does the verse promise God will fix my situation? It promises a strengthened heart, not a changed circumstance. Psalm 31 itself moves through unresolved trouble. Elsewhere Scripture affirms God's power to deliver, but this particular promise is about inner sustaining rather than external rescue.

What does it mean to "hope in the LORD"? Biblical hope is confident expectation grounded in God's character and promises, not optimism about how things will turn out. It's the difference between "I trust God" and "I trust that things will go my way." The first survives disappointment; the second doesn't.

Can I claim this verse if my faith feels weak right now? Yes. David wrote it after admitting he had felt cut off from God (v. 22). The verse is addressed to those who hope in the LORD, and weak hope is still hope directed at the right Person. Scripture elsewhere pictures God as gentle with fragile faith (Isaiah 42:3).
