Does the Bible Address Revenge? Here's What Scripture Says

By Bible Copilot Updated July 3, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards
Quick answer

Discover whether the Bible addresses revenge and what Scripture says about it. Learn biblical guidance for overcoming the desire for retaliation.

Does the Bible address revenge? Absolutely. Whether you're wondering if Scripture speaks to your struggle with revenge fantasies or seeking biblical guidance on justice, the Bible addresses revenge with clarity and compassion. Many believers suffer silently with the desire for revenge, unaware that Scripture speaks directly to this struggle. This guide answers: does the Bible address revenge, and if so, what exactly does it say?

Does the Bible Address Revenge Directly?

Romans 12:19 proves that yes, does the Bible address revenge with a direct command: "Do not take revenge, my dear friends, but leave room for God's wrath, for it is written: 'It is mine to avenge; I will repay,' says the Lord." Does the Bible address revenge? Emphatically yes, and with clear prohibition.

The fact that Paul addresses this shows does the Bible address revenge because it's a real temptation believers face. Does the Bible address revenge as a minor issue? No—it deserves direct apostolic instruction.

Does the Bible Address Revenge's Motivation?

Proverbs 20:22 shows does the Bible address revenge's root: "Do not say, 'I'll pay you back for this wrong!' Wait for the Lord, and he will avenge you." Does the Bible address revenge's motivation? Yes, it addresses the internal vow to retaliate.

Does the Bible address revenge by recognizing that the struggle isn't just about action—it's about the internal commitment to punish someone. Does the Bible address revenge by calling us to release even the intention to retaliate.

Does the Bible Address Revenge Through Christ's Example?

1 Peter 2:23 demonstrates that yes, does the Bible address revenge through Jesus: "When they hurled their insults at him, he did not retaliate; when he suffered, he made no threats. Instead, he entrusted himself to him who judges justly." Does the Bible address revenge? Yes, and it provides the ultimate model.

Does the Bible address revenge by showing that Christ—the most innocent person—chose not to pursue it. Does the Bible address revenge by calling us to follow His example.

Does the Bible Address Revenge's Alternative?

Matthew 5:38-40 shows does the Bible address revenge with an alternative: "You have heard that it was said, 'Eye for eye, and tooth for tooth.' But I tell you, do not resist an evil person. If someone strikes you on the right cheek, turn to him the other also." Does the Bible address revenge? Yes, and it offers grace instead.

Does the Bible address revenge by transcending the Old Testament principle of proportional justice. Does the Bible address revenge by calling for unexpected grace instead of retaliation.

Does the Bible Address Revenge's Practical Effects?

Proverbs 27:12 shows does the Bible address revenge's consequences: "The prudent see danger and take refuge, but the simple keep going and pay the penalty." Does the Bible address revenge? Yes, as something with real practical consequences.

Does the Bible address revenge by showing that pursuing retaliation leads to harm. Does the Bible address revenge by suggesting that the wise recognize this danger.

Does the Bible Address Revenge and God's Justice?

Hebrews 10:30 demonstrates does the Bible address revenge regarding justice: "For we know him who said, 'It is mine to avenge; I will repay.'" Does the Bible address revenge? Yes, with a promise that God will handle justice.

Does the Bible address revenge by removing the justification for retaliation. Does the Bible address revenge by assuring us that accountability doesn't depend on us.

Does the Bible Address Revenge and Prayer?

Matthew 5:44 shows does the Bible address revenge through the mechanism of prayer: "Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you." Does the Bible address revenge? Yes, with a practical tool for transformation.

Does the Bible address revenge by recognizing that prayer for someone's good is incompatible with wanting their harm. Does the Bible address revenge by providing prayer as the means of transformation.

Does the Bible Address Revenge and Forgiveness?

Colossians 3:13 demonstrates does the Bible address revenge through forgiveness: "Forgive as the Lord forgave you." Does the Bible address revenge? Yes, by connecting it to the forgiveness you've received.

Does the Bible address revenge by showing that seeking revenge contradicts the grace you've experienced. Does the Bible address revenge by pointing to forgiveness as the solution.

Does the Bible Address Revenge in Multiple Ways?

The beauty is that does the Bible address revenge comprehensively. It addresses through: - Direct command (Romans 12:19) - Christ's example (1 Peter 2:23) - Wisdom literature (Proverbs) - Prayer (Matthew 5:44) - Forgiveness (Colossians 3:13) - God's justice (Hebrews 10:30)

Does the Bible address revenge with multiple approaches because different people connect with different methods.

FAQ

Q: Does the Bible address revenge if someone deeply hurt me? A: Yes. Does the Bible address revenge acknowledging that even severe wrongs should not trigger retaliation. Does the Bible address revenge by calling for healing through God's grace instead.

Q: Does the Bible address revenge as sinful? A: Yes. Does the Bible address revenge as something that separates us from God and requires repentance and transformation.

Q: Can does the Bible address revenge without addressing justice? A: No. Does the Bible address revenge always in connection with God's justice system, assuring us that wrongs will be addressed.

Q: Does the Bible address revenge if it's justified? A: Does the Bible address revenge by forbidding personal retaliation regardless of justification. Does the Bible address revenge by calling us to trust God's justice instead.

Q: What's the first step in what does the Bible address about revenge? A: Does the Bible address revenge by calling you to release the person to God's justice. Speak forgiveness, pray for the person, and ask God for grace to let go of revenge.
