What Does the Bible Say About Friendship? (Complete Guide)

By Bible Copilot Updated July 3, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards
Quick answer

Complete guide to what the Bible says about friendship. Discover Scripture verses, biblical principles, and practical wisdom for Christian friendships.

Introduction

When Christians seek guidance on life's important relationships, the question naturally arises: What does the Bible say about friendship? This complete guide provides a comprehensive answer, drawing from Scripture's extensive wisdom on human connection, loyalty, and mutual growth. What the Bible says about friendship reveals a divine perspective far richer and deeper than popular culture offers. God addresses friendship throughout Scripture with remarkable intentionality, suggesting that these relationships matter profoundly to Him and should matter significantly to us.

The Bible says about friendship that it's a sacred bond deserving careful cultivation and faithful stewardship. From the Old Testament's wisdom literature to Jesus's teaching and Paul's epistles, what the Bible says about friendship encompasses multiple dimensions: the practical principles for building strong relationships, the spiritual benefits of meaningful friendships, the dangers of choosing companions unwisely, and the sacrificial love that characterizes true friendship. This complete guide explores each dimension, providing both theological grounding and practical application.

Understanding what the Bible says about friendship can transform how you approach relationships, helping you build connections that glorify God and contribute to your spiritual growth and the flourishing of your faith community.

The Core Teaching: What the Bible Says About Friendship

The Bible says about friendship, foundationally, that it's an essential part of human experience. Proverbs 17:17 provides a core statement: "A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity." What the Bible says about friendship in this verse captures two crucial elements: the constancy of true friendship and its particular value during hardship.

Proverbs 13:20 reveals another foundational principle: "Walk with the wise and become wise, for a companion of fools suffers harm." What the Bible says about friendship includes the reality that our friends shape us. They influence our thinking, our values, and our character development. This principle appears throughout Scripture because it's foundational to understanding why friendship matters.

Proverbs 27:17 expresses the transformative power of friendship: "As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another." What the Bible says about friendship through this metaphor is that relationships should refine and improve us. Friends help one another develop virtue, overcome weakness, and grow in wisdom.

Old Testament Wisdom on Friendship

The Old Testament, particularly Proverbs and Ecclesiastes, contains extensive teaching about what the Bible says about friendship. Proverbs 27:10 advises, "Do not forsake your friend or a friend of your family." What the Bible says about friendship includes a call to loyalty and consistency in our relationships. Friendships require intentional maintenance and presence.

Proverbs 27:12 addresses friendship's role in accountability: "Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but deceitful are the kisses of an enemy." What the Bible says about friendship suggests that true friends will sometimes tell you difficult truths because they care about your growth more than your momentary comfort. This kind of honest feedback, though sometimes painful, strengthens rather than weakens genuine friendship.

Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 provides practical wisdom: "Two are better than one, because they have a good return for their labor: If either of them falls down, one can help the other up. But pity anyone who falls and has no one to help them up." What the Bible says about friendship here acknowledges our human need for support, practical help, and companionship during difficult seasons.

David and Jonathan: The Model Friendship

Perhaps the most powerful biblical example illustrating what the Bible says about friendship appears in the relationship between David and Jonathan. 1 Samuel 18:1-3 describes their connection: "After David had finished talking with Saul, Jonathan became one in spirit with David, and he loved him as himself... And Jonathan made a covenant with David because he loved him as himself."

What the Bible says about friendship through their bond includes the concept of covenant—a binding commitment made before God. This wasn't casual companionship but a sacred agreement to remain faithful to one another. Jonathan, as the king's son, stood to lose his inheritance and position by befriending David, whom his father saw as a threat. Yet Jonathan chose loyalty to his friend over personal advancement.

1 Samuel 20:17 records, "And Jonathan had David reaffirm his oath out of love for him, because he loved him as he loved himself." What the Bible says about friendship reveals through their story the willingness to renew commitments, the joy of being fully known and accepted, and the courage required to maintain loyalty when circumstances become difficult.

Later, when they learned Saul intended to kill David, Jonathan helped David escape. 1 Samuel 20:42 captures their parting: "Jonathan said to David, 'Go in peace, for we have sworn friendship with each other in the name of the Lord.'" What the Bible says about friendship is embodied in this relationship: loyalty, sacrifice, and covenant made before God's witness.

Jesus's Revolutionary Teaching on Friendship

The New Testament presents revolutionary teaching about what the Bible says about friendship through Jesus Himself. In John 15:14-15, Jesus tells His disciples: "You are my friends if you do what I command. I no longer call you servants, because a servant does not know his master's business. Instead, I have called you friends, for everything that I have learned from my Father I have made known to you."

What the Bible says about friendship through Jesus includes the invitation to an unprecedented level of intimacy with God. Jesus doesn't maintain hierarchical distance from His disciples; instead, He calls them friends and shares everything He knows with them. This redefines friendship at the spiritual level.

John 15:12-13 provides what might be called the summary statement of Jesus's teaching on friendship: "My command is this: Love each other as I have loved you. Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends." What the Bible says about friendship is ultimately about sacrificial love—love willing to give everything for another's benefit.

New Testament Emphasis on Community and Encouragement

Paul's letters provide extensive teaching about what the Bible says about friendship within Christian community. 1 Thessalonians 5:11 exhorts: "Therefore encourage one another and build each other up, just as in fact you are doing." What the Bible says about friendship includes active, intentional encouragement as a spiritual practice.

Hebrews 10:24-25 emphasizes friendship's spiritual significance: "And let us consider how we may spur one another on toward love and good deeds, not giving up meeting together, as some are in the habit of doing, but encouraging one another—and all the more as you see the Day approaching." What the Bible says about friendship suggests that regular gathering and mutual encouragement are essential to spiritual growth.

Paul's personal relationships demonstrate what the Bible says about friendship in practice. His affection for Timothy, Titus, Philemon, and others shows that biblical leadership includes genuine friendships. Paul doesn't merely use these relationships; he values them for their own sake.

Choosing Friends Wisely: What the Bible Says

An essential aspect of what the Bible says about friendship concerns selection. Proverbs 22:24-25 warns: "Do not make friends with an angry man, do not associate with one easily angered, or you may learn his ways and get yourself ensnared." What the Bible says about friendship includes caution about the influence our closest relationships exert on our character.

Proverbs 13:20 reinforces this principle: "Walk with the wise and become wise, for a companion of fools suffers harm." What the Bible says about friendship suggests that we should choose friends who embody the character we hope to develop and who encourage us toward virtue.

For Christians specifically, 2 Corinthians 6:14 provides direction: "Do not be yoked together with unbelievers. For what do they have in common? Or what fellowship can light have with darkness?" While this verse addresses marriage, the principle applies to close friendships. What the Bible says about friendship suggests that deep, intimate relationships should be with those who share our spiritual commitments.

Honesty and Accountability in Friendship

What the Bible says about friendship includes the necessity of truthfulness and accountability. Proverbs 27:5-6 affirms: "Better is open rebuke than hidden love. Wounds from a friend can be trusted, but an enemy multiplies kisses." What the Bible says about friendship values honest feedback over empty flattery.

Proverbs 28:23 adds, "Whoever rebukes a person will in the end gain favor than one who has a flattering tongue." True friends care more about your growth than your approval. What the Bible says about friendship includes the courage to tell difficult truths and the humility to receive correction from those who love you.

Forgiveness and Reconciliation in Friendship

Since all friendships involve imperfect people, conflict is inevitable. Colossians 3:13 provides essential direction about what the Bible says about friendship: "Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you."

What the Bible says about friendship includes commitment to reconciliation and forgiveness. Matthew 18:15-17 outlines the process: address issues privately, seek understanding, and work toward restoration. Friendships often survive conflict and emerge stronger through the process of honest conversation and forgiveness.

FAQ

Q: What does the Bible say about the number of close friends you should have? A: The Bible doesn't specify a number. Jesus had twelve disciples but was closest to Peter, James, and John. What the Bible says about friendship emphasizes depth and quality over quantity. Invest deeply in a few meaningful relationships rather than spreading yourself thin.

Q: What does the Bible say about opposite-gender friendships? A: The Bible doesn't forbid them, but Scripture suggests caution regarding emotional intimacy and alone time, especially for married individuals. What the Bible says about friendship includes protecting existing commitments and maintaining transparency to guard relationships.

Q: Can you have a best friend as a Christian? A: Yes. David and Jonathan had a uniquely close bond; Jesus loved John particularly. What the Bible says about friendship doesn't discourage close relationships. However, ensure your closest friendship remains with God as your primary source of security.

Q: What does the Bible say about losing a friend? A: Psalm 55:12-14 expresses the pain of betrayal by a close friend. The Bible acknowledges that friendship loss grieves deeply. However, God remains faithful even when friends disappoint us, and He promises to never abandon us.

Q: How does what the Bible says about friendship apply today? A: Biblical principles about friendship—loyalty, honesty, mutual growth, and sacrificial love—transcend time and culture. While ancient friendships didn't have email or social media, the core dynamics remain timeless and highly applicable today.
