A Christian's Guide to Tithing: What the Bible Teaches

By Bible Copilot Updated July 3, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards
Quick answer

Learn what the Bible teaches about tithing with this Christian's guide. Explore scriptural principles and how to develop a biblical practice of giving tithes.

Introduction

A Christian's guide to tithing must be grounded in Scripture rather than cultural pressure or institutional expectation. What the Bible teaches about tithing presents a vision of proportional, faithful giving motivated by faith and gratitude rather than obligation. A Christian's guide to tithing helps you develop a biblically-informed practice that honors God and advances His kingdom. This guide explores what the Bible teaches about tithing, addresses common questions, and offers practical wisdom for implementation.

A Christian's Guide: What the Bible Teaches About Tithing

A Christian's guide to tithing begins with understanding the biblical foundation. What the Bible teaches about tithing in the Old Testament was clear: "A tithe of everything from the land, whether grain from the soil or fruit from the trees, belongs to the Lord; it is holy to the Lord" (Leviticus 27:30). What the Bible teaches about tithing was a mandatory practice with sacred significance.

What the Bible teaches about tithing shows multiple purposes. Numbers 18:21 explains that a Christian's guide to tithing included supporting Levites who had no land inheritance. Deuteronomy 14:28-29 shows what the Bible teaches about tithing's justice dimension—every third year, tithes went to vulnerable populations.

What the Bible teaches about tithing in the New Testament shifts emphasis. Jesus never commanded His followers to tithe. Instead, what the Bible teaches about tithing through Jesus emphasizes heart condition. Second Corinthians 9:7 captures what the Bible teaches about tithing: joyful, voluntary generosity motivated by grace, not compulsion.

A Christian's Guide: Understanding Tithing's Purpose

A Christian's guide to tithing requires understanding why we tithe. What the Bible teaches about tithing's purpose includes supporting God's work. Tithes provided for religious leaders and communal worship. A Christian's guide to tithing recognizes this historical purpose and contemporary equivalent—supporting your church and kingdom workers.

What the Bible teaches about tithing also includes protecting vulnerable populations. The tithe system built justice into economic life. A Christian's guide to tithing might include directing gifts toward the poor and marginalized.

What the Bible teaches about tithing includes developing spiritual maturity. Deuteronomy 14:23 states that tithes helped believers "learn to revere the Lord your God always." The practice itself teaches faith and trust. A Christian's guide to tithing recognizes that the discipline of regular, proportional giving shapes our relationship with God and possessions.

A Christian's Guide: From Obligation to Grace

A Christian's guide to tithing must navigate the shift from Old Testament law to New Testament grace. The Old Testament commanded tithing; the New Testament doesn't. A Christian's guide to tithing suggests that the principle—proportional, faithful giving—remains vital, though application varies.

What the Bible teaches about tithing through Paul is that generosity flows from grace. Second Corinthians 8:1-7 describes churches giving "overflowing joy and their extreme poverty welled up in rich generosity." A Christian's guide to tithing recognizes that grace, not law, motivates authentic generosity.

What the Bible teaches about tithing shows that many Christians find the ten percent framework helpful. A Christian's guide to tithing acknowledges that using tithing as a standard provides clarity and ensures meaningful giving. Others give differently based on conviction and capacity. A Christian's guide to tithing emphasizes that what matters is faithful, proportional, joyful giving.

A Christian's Guide: The Heart Motivation

A Christian's guide to tithing must emphasize that motivation matters supremely. What the Bible teaches about tithing through 2 Corinthians 9:7 is that God loves the cheerful giver. Tithing should flow from joy and willing conviction, not guilt or obligation.

A Christian's guide to tithing recognizes that examining our hearts is essential. Why do we tithe? Do we seek recognition? Are we fulfilling obligation? Or are we responding to grace and trusting God? The widow's offering in Mark 12:41-44 shows what the Bible teaches about tithing—Jesus evaluated generosity by sacrifice and sincerity, not amount.

What the Bible teaches about tithing also addresses the danger of pharisaism. Matthew 23:23 records Jesus critiquing those who tithed meticulously while neglecting justice and mercy. A Christian's guide to tithing balances giving proportionally with pursuing justice and showing mercy.

A Christian's Guide: Trust and Provision

A Christian's guide to tithing requires faith in God's provision. What the Bible teaches about tithing through Matthew 6:25-34 is that Jesus invites disciples not to worry about provision. Jesus promises that seeking God's kingdom produces provision: "All these things will be given to you as well."

What the Bible teaches about tithing assumes that believers can give generously because God cares for their needs. Philippians 4:19 assures: "And my God will meet all your needs according to the riches of his glory in Christ Jesus." A Christian's guide to tithing rests on this promise.

Luke 6:38 captures what the Bible teaches about tithing: "Give, and it will be given to you. A good measure, pressed down, shaken together and running over, will be poured into your lap." A Christian's guide to tithing recognizes that giving produces return—though return may take forms beyond financial gain.

A Christian's Guide: Proportional and Planned

A Christian's guide to tithing should be proportional and planned. What the Bible teaches about tithing through 1 Corinthians 16:1-2 is Paul's recommendation: "On the first day of every week, each one of you should set aside a sum of money in keeping with your income." A Christian's guide to tithing includes regular, planned giving based on capacity.

What the Bible teaches about tithing through 2 Corinthians 8:12 reassures those with limited means: "For if the willingness is there, the gift is acceptable according to what one has, not according to what one does not have." A Christian's guide to tithing removes shame from modest giving and emphasizes that proportional giving from struggle is valuable.

A Christian's Guide: Stewardship and Accountability

A Christian's guide to tithing includes understanding stewardship. What the Bible teaches about tithing through 1 Peter 4:10 is that we're faithful stewards of God's grace. Tithing reflects this understanding—we manage God's resources for His purposes.

What the Bible teaches about tithing shows that ultimate accountability belongs to God. Romans 14:12 states: "So then, each of us will give an account of ourselves to God." A Christian's guide to tithing recognizes that we're accountable for how we use God's gifts.

A Christian's Guide: Practical Implementation

A Christian's guide to tithing suggests:

Pray about your giving: Ask God to guide your decisions about percentage and allocation.

Calculate based on capacity: Determine what percentage or amount your finances support. Give what you can with joy.

Direct thoughtfully: Consider supporting your church, the poor, missions, and justice causes. Balance support across priorities.

Plan systematically: Set aside giving money regularly rather than spontaneously. Consistency demonstrates commitment.

Give with grace: Remember that what the Bible teaches about tithing is that God values willing, joyful generosity. Don't give resentfully or beyond capacity.

FAQ

Q: What the Bible teaches about tithing—should I give ten percent? A: Many Christians find ten percent helpful. What the Bible teaches about tithing in the New Testament emphasizes proportional, joyful giving. Give what you can based on conviction and capacity.

Q: What the Bible teaches about tithing—if I'm struggling financially? A: What the Bible teaches about tithing accepts proportional giving. Give what you can. As circumstances improve, increase giving. God values the willing heart.

Q: What the Bible teaches about tithing—where should it go? A: Traditionally, tithes supported religious leadership and community. Today, a Christian's guide to tithing might direct tithes to your church, missions, the poor, or organizations advancing God's kingdom.

Q: What the Bible teaches about tithing—should I tithe before or after taxes? A: What the Bible teaches about tithing addresses pre-tax giving in an agricultural context. Prayerfully decide based on your circumstances. The principle—proportional, faithful giving—remains constant.

Q: What the Bible teaches about tithing—if my church misuses funds? A: What the Bible teaches about tithing emphasizes prayerful discernment. You can direct tithes to organizations aligned with biblical priorities while supporting your church in other ways.
