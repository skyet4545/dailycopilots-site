1 Corinthians 6:19–20 Meaning: Your Body as a Temple

By Bible Copilot Updated October 1, 2026 Scripture-cited · editorial standards

Quick answer

In 1 Corinthians 6:19–20, Paul calls believers to honor God with their bodies because they belong to Christ and have received the Holy Spirit. Read with verses 12–18, the temple image is part of his argument against sexual immorality. It is not a test of physical appearance or a promise that faithful people will never become ill.

Read the whole argument

Start with 1 Corinthians 6:12–20 rather than lifting the final two verses out of the paragraph. Paul connects bodily conduct with belonging to the Lord, resurrection and union with Christ. His conclusion follows from those relationships: what believers do with their bodies matters.

Three questions help trace the passage:

  1. What reasons does Paul give for saying the body belongs to the Lord? Read verses 13–14.
  2. How do verses 15–18 connect belonging to Christ with bodily conduct?
  3. What does the Spirit's presence add to the appeal in verses 19–20?

The temple image and God's presence

Paul's image draws attention to God's presence and the holiness of what belongs to him. A related passage, 1 Corinthians 3:16–17, addresses the church as God's temple. Reading both passages helps you ask how Paul uses the image in different arguments. Do not assume that every occurrence is making exactly the same point.

The metaphor does not make health, strength or appearance a measure of spiritual worth. The text gives no basis for saying that disability or illness means someone is less valuable to God.

What does “bought at a price” mean?

The purchase language emphasizes belonging and obligation. In Christian reading, it points to Christ's saving work; compare 1 Corinthians 7:23 and 1 Peter 1:18–19. Paul uses it to call believers to honor God. The phrase should not be turned into a claim that another person owns or may control your body.

Is this verse mainly about diet and exercise?

No. Its immediate subject is sexual conduct and belonging to Christ. Christians may reflect more broadly on caring for their bodies, but those applications should be distinguished from Paul's immediate argument. This passage is not a medical guide or a way to judge someone's body.

A simple way to apply the passage

Read the whole paragraph, identify Paul's reasons, then name one response appropriate to the text. An honest response begins with attention to God and other people, rather than shame about appearance or comparison with someone else's health. Bring difficult personal or pastoral questions to a trusted, qualified person as well as studying the passage.

Sources and study boundary

Read 1 Corinthians 6:12–20, 3:16–17 and 7:23, and 1 Peter 1:18–19. This explanation is a study aid. Scripture remains primary; compare interpretations with trusted sources and pastoral guidance.

  • 1 Corinthians 6:12–20
  • 1 Corinthians 3:16–17
  • 1 Corinthians 7:23
  • 1 Peter 1:18–19
