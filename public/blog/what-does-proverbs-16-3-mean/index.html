What Does Proverbs 16:3 Mean? "Commit Your Work to the Lord"

📖 Bible Copilot ⏱ ~3 min read

Short answer: Proverbs 16:3 teaches that when we entrust our work and plans to God, He steadies and directs them: "Commit your deeds to Yahweh, and your plans shall succeed" (WEB); the KJV reads, "Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established." To "commit" is to roll the whole weight of your efforts onto God, submitting them to His will — and the promise is that plans surrendered to Him will be firmly established, not that God rubber-stamps whatever we want.

The context

Proverbs is a collection of wisdom sayings, largely from Solomon, teaching how to live skillfully under God. Chapter 16 opens with several verses on the relationship between human planning and God's sovereignty. Verse 1: "The plans of the heart belong to man, but the answer of the tongue is from the LORD." Verse 9: "A man's heart plans his course, but the LORD directs his steps." Verse 3 sits inside this cluster. Read together, they teach a balance: we genuinely plan and work, yet God governs the outcome. Verse 3 tells us what to do with our plans — commit them to God.

What it means, phrase by phrase

  • "Commit your deeds / works" — The Hebrew image is literally "roll" your works onto the LORD, like rolling a heavy load onto someone stronger. It means handing over your efforts, trusting God with them rather than carrying the anxiety alone.
  • "to the LORD / Yahweh" — The commitment is directed to God specifically, which implies aligning your work with His will, not merely wishing for success.
  • "your plans shall be established / succeed" — "Established" means made firm, steady, secure. The promise is stability and right direction for plans genuinely surrendered to God.

Read alongside verses 1 and 9, this is not a formula for guaranteeing personal ambitions. It is a promise that when we submit our work to God, He shapes and steadies it — sometimes redirecting our plans toward what is truly good.

Cross-references

  • Psalm 37:5 — "Commit your way to the LORD. Trust also in him, and he will do this."
  • Proverbs 3:5–6 — trust God and He will make your paths straight.
  • Philippians 4:6–7 — bring your requests to God and His peace will guard you.
  • James 4:13–15 — plan humbly, saying "if the Lord wills."

How to apply it today

Proverbs 16:3 is practical for anyone facing a decision, project, career move, or big undertaking. Before you push ahead on your own strength, "roll" the work onto God in prayer — genuinely offering it to Him and asking that His will, not just yours, be done. This does not replace planning and effort; it frames them. It also loosens anxiety: committing your work to God means you are not solely responsible for controlling the outcome. Over time, this habit reshapes your ambitions, so that the plans you bring are increasingly ones God can establish.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Proverbs 16:3 guarantee my plans will succeed? It promises that plans truly committed to God will be established, but read with verses 1 and 9 it also means God directs the outcome. Committing your work to Him may lead Him to steady, redirect, or reshape your plans toward what is genuinely good rather than simply granting whatever you wanted.

What does it mean to "commit" your work? The Hebrew pictures rolling a heavy load onto someone stronger. To commit your work is to hand it over to God in trust and submission, rather than carrying the full weight and anxiety of it yourself.

Does this verse mean I don't need to plan or work hard? No. Proverbs elsewhere praises diligence and wise planning. This verse addresses the posture of the heart — planning and working while entrusting the results to God.

How is Proverbs 16:3 different from Proverbs 3:5–6? They are closely related. Proverbs 3:5–6 emphasizes trusting God rather than your own understanding; Proverbs 16:3 emphasizes committing your specific works and plans to Him. Both call for surrendering control to God.
