What Does Psalm 100:4 Mean? Enter His Gates With Thanksgiving

📖 Bible Copilot ⏱ ~3 min read

Short answer: Psalm 100:4 means we should come into God's presence with grateful, praising hearts — approaching Him thankfully and blessing His name. It pictures worship that begins with thanksgiving, reminding us that gratitude is the doorway into meeting God.

The context: a psalm of joyful worship

Psalm 100 is a short, exuberant call to worship, headed "A Psalm of praise" (or thanksgiving). Its five verses summon all the earth to serve the Lord with gladness and come before Him with singing. The psalm was likely used as worshipers approached the temple. Verse 4 pictures that literal approach — passing through the gates and into the courts — and verse 5 gives the reason for all the praise: "the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting." Thanksgiving here is grounded in who God is.

What it means, phrase by phrase

The King James Version reads: "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name" (Psalm 100:4).

  • "Enter into his gates... into his courts" — the temple imagery of drawing near to God. To enter is to come into His presence deliberately.
  • "with thanksgiving... with praise" — these describe the manner of approach. We do not come grumbling or distracted but grateful and praising.
  • "be thankful unto him" — gratitude directed to God as the giver of every good thing.
  • "bless his name" — to speak well of God, to honor who He is. His "name" stands for His character and reputation.

The verse teaches that worship has a proper tone. Gratitude is not an optional add-on but the very posture in which we come to God — because He is good and His mercy endures forever (v. 5).

Cross-references

  • Psalm 100:5 — "the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting" (KJV) — the reason for thanks.
  • Psalm 95:2 — "come before his presence with thanksgiving" (KJV).
  • 1 Thessalonians 5:18 — "In every thing give thanks" (KJV).
  • Colossians 3:16 — Sing "with grace in your hearts to the Lord" (KJV).

How to apply Psalm 100:4 today

Under the new covenant, believers come to God directly through Christ, but the posture of this verse still shapes worship. Applying it means beginning prayer and worship with thanksgiving rather than only requests — naming what God has done before asking for more. It also reframes gratitude as an entryway: when you feel far from God or weighed down, deliberately thanking Him and blessing His name can turn your heart toward His presence. Gratitude is not pretending everything is fine; it is remembering that God is good and His mercy never runs out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Psalm 100:4 mention gates and courts? These refer to the temple in Jerusalem, where worshipers literally entered God's presence. The imagery pictures deliberately drawing near to God — something believers now do through Christ — with thanksgiving as the way we approach.

What does it mean to "bless his name"? To bless God's name is to speak well of Him and honor His character. His "name" represents who He is — good, merciful, faithful — so blessing His name means praising Him for that.

Why start worship with thanksgiving? Because gratitude reorients the heart toward God's goodness before we bring our needs. Psalm 100 grounds the thanks in verse 5 — God is good and His mercy is everlasting — giving worshipers a solid reason to enter His presence thankfully.
