What are the lower and higher natures in the Bhagavad Gita?

In chapter 7, verses 4 and 5, Krishna introduces a distinction between two aspects of his own nature (prakriti) that together account for the entire universe. Verse 4 describes the lower nature (apara prakriti): "Earth, water, fire, air, ether, mind, intellect, and false ego - these eight constitute My separated material energies." This eightfold division covers the whole of the physical and psychological apparatus through which ordinary experience happens: the five gross elements, plus mind (manas), intellect (buddhi), and the sense of separate selfhood (ahankara) that identifies a person with their body and circumstances. Verse 5 then names something distinct: "Besides these, O mighty-armed Arjuna, there is another, superior energy of Mine, which comprises the living entities who are exploiting the resources of this material, inferior nature." This higher nature (para prakriti) is consciousness itself - the animating life-force present in every living being, described as superior precisely because it is what makes the eightfold material nature usable and meaningful in the first place; matter alone, however elaborately organized, cannot know or experience anything without it. The distinction matters practically because the Gita's broader teaching on liberation depends on it: confusing oneself with the lower, material nature - one's body, mind, and ego - is treated as the basic error that produces suffering and bondage, while recognizing oneself with the higher nature, the conscious self connected to the Supreme, is the beginning of spiritual clarity. This same two-nature framework reappears in chapter 13's discussion of kshetra (the field, corresponding to lower nature) and kshetrajna (the knower of the field, corresponding to higher nature), showing the Gita returning to this same basic map of reality from a different angle later in the text.
Source: Bhagavad Gita 7.4-5
