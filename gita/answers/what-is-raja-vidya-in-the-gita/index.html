What does raja vidya mean in the Bhagavad Gita?

Raja vidya raja guhyam is the phrase that opens the Bhagavad Gita's ninth chapter, and Krishna uses it to introduce what he calls the most confidential teaching he will give Arjuna. In verse 9.1, Krishna tells Arjuna, addressed as free of envy (anasuyave), that he will now reveal the most secret knowledge (guhyatamam) together with direct realization (vijnana), knowing which will free him from the inauspicious, ashubhat. Verse 9.2 names this teaching raja-vidya raja-guhyam, the king of knowledge and the king of secrets, pavitram idam uttamam, the purest and highest, directly perceptible (pratyaksavagamam), in accordance with dharma, very easy to practice, and imperishable (avyayam). The chapter goes on to describe Krishna's presence as pervading the entire universe while remaining unattached to it, sustaining all beings without being contained by them, the way vast wind moves constantly through space yet always rests within it. What makes this teaching royal rather than merely useful, per the chapter's own framing, is that it is not restricted to ascetics or ritual specialists: verse 9.2 stresses that it is su-sukham, very easy to practice, open to anyone who approaches it with faith rather than requiring years of preparation. That claim of accessibility, paired with the description of Krishna as the sustaining, all-pervading ground of existence, is what distinguishes chapter 9 from the Gita's earlier chapters on action and knowledge: it is presented as the most direct route to the same liberation those other paths describe.
Source: Bhagavad Gita 9.1-9.2
