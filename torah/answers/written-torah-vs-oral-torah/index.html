What is the difference between the Written Torah and the Oral Torah?

If you want the shortest answer: the Written Torah is the fixed text, and the Oral Torah is the interpretive tradition that explains how that text is learned and lived.

Written Torah means the text itself, most narrowly the Five Books of Moses. Oral Torah means the transmitted interpretation that explains how those commandments are understood, limited, and practiced.

That is why Jewish sources treat the distinction as more than “book versus commentary.” The written text often speaks briefly. It commands, names, or forbids, but it does not always spell out every practical case. The Oral Torah carries the chain of explanation that turns those brief commands into a livable pattern of study and observance.

Later rabbinic works preserve that tradition in written form. The Mishnah is the early compilation; the Gemara is the later layer of analysis around it; together they form the Talmud. So Written Torah versus Oral Torah is not exactly the same question as Torah versus Talmud, even though the topics overlap closely.

Quick comparison

Written Torah
The Five Books of Moses as fixed written text.
Oral Torah
The interpretive tradition that explains how the written text is taught and practiced.
Later records
The Mishnah and the Gemara preserve central parts of Oral Torah in written form.
Why both matter
The written text is often too brief to settle every practical question on its own.

Source map: text, transmission, and application

Written text
Deuteronomy 31
Deuteronomy describes Moses writing down the Torah and entrusting it to Israel. That is the written-text side of the distinction.
Transmission
Pirkei Avot 1:1
Pirkei Avot opens with a chain of transmission from Sinai onward, a classic rabbinic way of naming how Torah teaching is passed down beyond the written scroll alone.
Practical need
Deuteronomy 6:8
The command to bind these words “as a sign on your hand” names an obligation but does not explain every practical detail. Jewish tradition treats questions like this as one reason Oral Torah is necessary.
Rabbinic record
Mishnah and Gemara
The Talmud is built from the Mishnah and the Gemara. It is not the whole of Oral Torah, but it is one of its main written witnesses.

How this differs from Torah vs. Talmud

Written Torah vs. Oral Torah asks about the relationship between text and interpretive tradition. Torah vs. Talmud asks about the written foundation versus one major rabbinic textual record of how that foundation is debated and applied.
